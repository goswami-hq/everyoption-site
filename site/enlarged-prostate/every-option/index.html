How to read this page

The studies behind the short guide. Each treatment starts with the conclusion and how sure we are, in the short guide's words, then the studies, harms and what is not known.

Numbers from different studies are not head-to-head comparisons. They come from different men, follow-up times, and outcome definitions, so this page never lines them up as if they did.

A number read only in a study's abstract, not its full paper, is marked (abstract only).

When a study gives a range after a number, the true result most likely falls inside that range. A wide range means the study could not pin the result down.

Symptom scores are the IPSS, where higher is worse. Erection scores are the IIEF or SHIM, where higher is better.

Funding is named where we found it; where it is not named, it was not checked.

What is an enlarged prostate?

The prostate is a small gland that wraps around the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the body. In almost all men, it grows with age. It can squeeze the urethra and make it hard to empty the bladder. As men get older, this gets more common, with more than 9 out of 10 men having it in their 80s.2, 3

Common symptoms2, 3

  • Frequent peeing
  • A strong, sudden urge to go
  • Getting up at night to pee
  • A weak or slow stream
  • Straining
  • Dribbling at the end
When to see a doctor

See a doctor if your bladder does not feel empty after you pee, or if self-care has not helped after 2 months. A doctor also checks for other causes, such as infection or cancer.2, 3

BPH medicines compared

These ease symptoms like frequent peeing and a weak stream. No procedure is needed.

Alpha blockers

How well it works
Good evidence suggests Ease symptoms for many men; most notice a change within 3 to 7 days
What it involves
A daily pill
Recovery
None
Sex life
Ejaculation may change

Tamsulosin and similar pills relax the muscle in the prostate and where the bladder meets it (the bladder neck).2, 3

Bottom line

Good evidence suggests alpha blockers ease symptoms for many men, and most notice a change within 3 to 7 days.2, 3

The studies

  • Dizziness and ejaculation changes were more common than on a placebo. In the drug label's trials, men taking tamsulosin 0.4 mg were compared with men taking a placebo (a pill with no active ingredient). Dizziness: 14.9% on tamsulosin against 10.1% on placebo. A change in ejaculation (abnormal ejaculation): 8.4% against 0.2%.4

Harms, and how they were counted

Alpha blockers can cause dizziness on standing. They can also change ejaculation (less or no semen). The label tells men planning cataract or glaucoma surgery to tell their eye surgeon, because of a floppy-iris problem during the operation.4

Who was studied, and who it is for

Alpha blockers are usually the first medicine tried for bothersome symptoms. They treat symptoms, not the growth. So on their own, they do not lower the chance of retention (being unable to pass urine) or surgery.3, 5

Prostate-shrinking pills

How well it works
Strong evidence shows Lower the chance of surgery: finasteride cut it about in half over 4 years
What it involves
A daily pill, for months
Recovery
None
Sex life
Lower sex drive or erection problems can happen

Finasteride and dutasteride block an enzyme (a body chemical) that turns testosterone into another male hormone, dihydrotestosterone. The prostate then slowly shrinks. It takes 3 to 6 months to feel the effect.2, 7

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows they lower the chance of retention and surgery in men with enlarged prostates. Finasteride cut the chance of surgery about in half over 4 years. Adding one of these pills to an alpha blocker helps more than an alpha blocker alone in larger prostates.5– to 9

The studies

  • About half as many men needed surgery on finasteride. In the PLESS trial, 3,040 men with enlarged prostates were assigned by chance (randomized) to finasteride or a placebo for 4 years. Surgery: 69 of 1,513 men on finasteride (5%) against 152 of 1,503 on placebo (10%) (abstract only).6
  • Two pills together cut the chance of getting worse. The MTOPS trial assigned 3,047 men by chance to a placebo, doxazosin (an alpha blocker), finasteride, or both pills, for about 4.5 years on average. Getting worse was counted as a symptom score rise of 4 points or more, being unable to pass urine (retention), leaking urine, kidney problems (kidney insufficiency), or repeat infections. Men taking both pills were 66% less likely to get worse than men taking a placebo. Both pills together, and finasteride alone, lowered the chance of retention and of surgery or another procedure (invasive treatment) (abstract only).5
  • Two pills eased symptoms more than one, but did not lower surgery more than dutasteride alone. In the CombAT trial, funded by a drug maker, dutasteride plus tamsulosin eased symptoms more than either drug alone. However, it did not lower the chance of retention or surgery more than dutasteride alone.9

Harms, and how they were counted

These pills can lower sex drive, cause erection problems, and mean less semen. They cut the PSA level (a blood test used to check for prostate cancer) about in half, so a PSA result must be read with that in mind. Both drug labels warn they may raise the chance of a more serious (higher-grade) prostate cancer.7, 8

Who was studied, and who it is for

The trials enrolled men with enlarged prostates.5, 6

Tadalafil

How well it works
Early studies suggest Symptom score 1.89 points better than placebo over 6 to 12 weeks, a small effect
What it involves
A daily pill
Recovery
None
Sex life
Also used for erection problems

A daily low dose of tadalafil, the erection pill, relaxes muscle in the bladder and prostate.3, 11

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it eases symptoms a little. The pooled effect is small and the evidence is rated low.10, 11

The studies

  • Symptoms eased a little more than with a placebo, and side effects were more common. A Cochrane review (a review that pools the trials) of these pills found that symptom scores (IPSS) improved 1.89 points more than with a placebo (range 1.50 to 2.27) over 6 to 12 weeks. The evidence is rated low certainty. Men taking the pills were more likely to have side effects (adverse events) than men taking a placebo (risk ratio 1.42).10

Harms, and how they were counted

Tadalafil can cause headache, indigestion, and back pain. It must not be taken with nitrate heart medicines. The label also does not recommend it with alpha blockers for this use.11

Who was studied, and who it is for

It may suit men who also want help with erections (our inference; it is also an erection pill).11

Is waiting safe?

For most men without retention, kidney damage, or bladder stones, the evidence suggests that waiting and stepping up treatment is reasonable.3, 12

  • In one older trial, TURP meant fewer bad outcomes than waiting. A Veterans Affairs trial assigned 556 men with moderate symptoms by chance to TURP or watchful waiting. Treatment failure (the trial's set of bad outcomes) happened in 23 men after TURP against 47 men who waited: men who had TURP were about half as likely to have treatment fail (risk ratio 0.48). Of the waiting men, 24% had surgery within 3 years (abstract only).12
  • Its authors call waiting usually a safe choice for men less bothered by symptoms.12
  • The trial enrolled men decades ago, before today's medicines and procedures.12

This does not apply to men already in retention, with kidney damage, or with bladder stones. They need treatment, often surgery.1, 3

PAE vs. other less invasive treatments

These treat the prostate without major surgery. Nothing is cut out of your body. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Prostate artery embolization (PAE)

Does insurance cover it? →

How well it works
Early results are encouraging Symptom score 13.2 points better than a sham at 6 months, in one trial (abstract only); against TURP, a gap that matters has not been ruled out
What it involves
A thin tube through a tiny skin nick to the prostate arteries
Recovery
Most go home the same day; back to normal within a few days
Sex life
Most keep normal ejaculation; erections do not seem affected

An interventional radiologist passes a thin tube (catheter) from the wrist or groin into the arteries that feed the prostate. The man gets numbing medicine at the skin (local anesthetic) and light sedation. Small particles are injected to cut the gland's blood supply, so it shrinks. Most men do not need a urine tube. In a UK registry (a national record of treated men), 71% of PAE procedures were done without an overnight stay. In contrast, 80% of men having TURP stayed in hospital at least 1 night (abstract only).22, 25, 28

Bottom line

Early results are encouraging. Against a sham, PAE clearly eased symptoms in the short term, in one trial. Against TURP, symptom relief in the first year or two may be close. However, a gap that matters has not been ruled out, TURP opens the urine channel more, and more men need a second procedure after PAE. Against two pills in men with larger prostates, two small trials favour PAE (in both, the men knew which treatment they had).13– to 17

The studies

  • Against a sham, PAE clearly eased symptoms in the short term. One trial compared PAE with a sham (a fake procedure) in 80 men with severe symptoms. It ran at one private clinic, led by the group that developed the technique. At 6 months, symptom scores were 13.2 points better after PAE than after the sham (range 10.2 to 16.2). The Cochrane review's figure for the same trial is 12.07 points better (range 8.69 to 15.45), rated moderate certainty. Up to 6 months, the men did not know which they had had (blinded). After that, men who had the sham could switch to PAE (abstract only).13, 14
  • Against TURP, pooled: close in the first two years, but a gap that matters is not ruled out. A Cochrane review searched for trials up to November 2021 and pooled them. The men's prostates averaged 72.8 mL. Up to 12 months (6 randomized trials, 360 men), symptom scores were 1.72 points better after TURP than after PAE (range -0.37 to 3.81), low certainty. At 24 months (2 trials, 176 men), they were 2.58 points better after TURP (range -1.54 to 6.71), low certainty. Both ranges include 3 points, the smallest difference men notice, so a gap that matters has not been ruled out.14
  • The best single trial against TURP: symptoms eased somewhat more after TURP, with fewer harms after PAE. The Swiss trial assigned 103 men at one hospital by chance to PAE or TURP. Both the men and their doctors knew which treatment was given (open label). At 12 weeks, after adjusting for differences between the groups, symptom scores were 2.87 points better after TURP than after PAE (range 0.53 to 5.21). Harms were counted the same way in both groups, as they happened, with a standard grading system (modified Clavien) and independent monitoring. There were 36 harms (adverse events) after PAE against 70 after TURP. Two or more harms: 16.7% of men after PAE against 45.1% after TURP. Severe pain in the first 12 to 24 hours was more common after PAE (18.8% against 4.3%), but this difference could be due to chance (p = 0.06).15
  • Five years later, too few men were left to be sure; urine flow was better after TURP. At 5 years in the same trial, only 18 of 48 men in the PAE group and 38 of 51 in the TURP group were still being followed. Symptom scores had improved 7.78 points after PAE and 11.57 after TURP. The difference, 3.79 points, could be due to chance (range -0.66 to 8.24; p = 0.092). Urine flow rose more after TURP: 3.59 after PAE against 9.30 mL/s after TURP (abstract only).18
  • Against two pills, PAE eased symptoms more. The PARTEM trial assigned 90 men by chance to PAE or to two pills (dutasteride plus tamsulosin). Their prostates were 50 mL or more, and they were still bothered by symptoms on an alpha blocker. The men knew which treatment they had (open label). At 9 months, symptom scores had improved 10.0 points after PAE against 5.7 points on the pills (difference 4.4; range 1.9 to 6.9). It was funded by the French Ministry of Health, with a grant from a maker of embolization materials named in the abstract (abstract only).16
  • A second small trial against two pills also favoured PAE. The P-EASY ADVANCE trial assigned 39 men by chance to PAE or to two pills (tamsulosin plus dutasteride). The men had no earlier treatment, and their prostates were about 86 mL. The men knew which treatment they had (open label). On a bladder pressure and flow test, 63% after PAE against 28% on the pills were no longer blocked (p = 0.03). Symptom scores also improved more after PAE (abstract only).17
  • In very large prostates, open surgery relieved the blockage more. The PoPAE trial assigned 48 men with prostates over 80 mL by chance to PAE (23 men) or to open surgery that removes the inner prostate (simple prostatectomy; 25 men), and followed them for 6 months. Symptom scores and the urine left in the bladder improved about equally. However, urine flow rose 8.3 after PAE against 15.1 mL/s after surgery. On a bladder pressure and flow test, 70% of men after PAE were still blocked, against 12% after open surgery (abstract only).19
  • Without a comparison group, symptoms stayed better for years in large series.
    • 630 men in a row were treated at one centre and followed, with no comparison group. By the authors' own definition of success, PAE worked for 81.9% at 1 to 3 years and 76.3% at more than 3 to 6.5 years (abstract only).20
    • A study across several countries followed 478 men from the time of treatment (405 with symptoms, 73 in retention). A maker of embolization materials funded it. Symptom scores were 21.8 before and 11.2 at 24 months. Of the men in retention, 65.8% became and stayed free of a catheter. Any harm: 11.5% of men. Serious harm: 2.1%.21

Harms, and how they were counted

  • Common and short-lived: a burning feeling in the lower belly, peeing often, and pelvic pain for a few days. A little blood in the urine or stool can happen.22, 23
  • Serious problems: too uncertain to compare with TURP. In the Cochrane review, serious harms (major adverse events) showed no clear difference between PAE and TURP, and any gap could be due to chance: risk ratio 0.75 up to 12 months (range 0.19 to 2.97; 4 trials, 250 men) and 0.91 at 13 to 24 months (range 0.20 to 4.05; 2 trials, 206 men). Each range runs from much less likely to several times as likely. Both are very low certainty.14
  • In other studies, serious-harm rates depend on who counted and how. A Danish study followed 336 men treated across the country, from the time of treatment (a prospective cohort). The typical (median) prostate was 101 mL, and 26% of the men depended on a urine tube. Serious harms (serious adverse events): 7.7%. Repeat treatment: 9.5% at 2 years and 18% at 5 years. Of the men who depended on a tube, 60% were free of it within 6 months (abstract only). The study across several countries above gives its own rate.21, 24
  • Severe harms from particles reaching the bladder, rectum, or penis have been reported. They are rare and appear mainly as case reports, with no reliable rate.14, 21
  • How harms were counted matters. Only the Swiss trial graded both groups the same way. Older trials may have under-reported harms after TURP. So rates from PAE studies with no comparison group cannot be set beside rates from surgery studies.15

Who was studied, and who it is for

The trials enrolled men fit for either treatment, with an average prostate around the size in the pooled review. Men who have PAE in practice are often older, with larger prostates. They may be in retention, on blood thinners, or unwilling or unable to have surgery, as in the Danish study above. That cuts both ways (our inference). Sicker men raise the rates of harm and failure, while motivated, well-counselled men raise the rate of symptom relief. None of the studies without a comparison group (cohorts) adjusted for frailty.14, 24, 25

In retention, studies without a comparison group report that most men become free of a catheter: 1,075 men at one U.S. centre, looking back at their records, followed for 458 days on average. Harms were graded with the Society of Interventional Radiology system. Of the men eligible for a repeat treatment, 16% had one, up to 60 months. Of 126 men in retention, 94% were free of a catheter at 3 months (abstract only). No trial was found that assigned catheter-dependent men by chance to PAE or surgery.21, 26

What experts say

The American Urological Association's 2026 guideline gives a conditional recommendation (doctors may offer it), evidence grade B, for PAE with particles in prostates of 50 cc or more. It says symptom relief can be about as much as after TURP for up to 2 years, with more repeat treatment than after TURP. It advises a urologist's assessment before PAE, and PAE done by interventional radiologists trained in it. It says doctors may offer PAE, among other options, to men on blood thinners or at high risk of bleeding. It says men should be told that PAE exposes them to radiation. For men who depend on a catheter, it recommends surgery that removes tissue.1, 14

What is not known

  • Whether PAE spares surgery or only puts it off: no trial reports the time to surgery.14, 27
  • Surgery after PAE: PAE leaves surgery open. Small series of surgery after PAE (robotic, simple prostatectomy, TURP) show no sign of harm, but there is no good evidence either way.14, 27
  • How it compares with laser removal of the inner prostate (HoLEP): no randomized trial.14, 18
  • Long-term results against a randomized comparison group: the one long trial lost track of most men in the PAE group.14, 18
  • Which particle and size work best: not settled.14, 18

Urethral lift

How well it works
Early studies suggest Eases symptoms, often within 2 weeks; about 1 out of 7 men need another procedure within 5 years
What it involves
Implants placed through the penis
Recovery
About 1 week to normal activity
Sex life
Usually unchanged

Small implants, placed through a scope in the urethra (the tube that carries urine out of the body), pull the prostate lobes apart like curtain tiebacks. Nothing is removed.2, 3, 31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest the lift eases symptoms, often within 2 weeks, in prostates of the size tested. In its trial, about 1 out of 7 men need another procedure within 5 years.29

The studies

  • Some men needed more surgery within five years. 206 men with prostates of 30 to 80 cc were assigned by chance to the lift or a sham. They were then followed to 5 years with no comparison group. The maker paid for the trial. Over 5 years, 13.6% needed more surgery (surgical retreatment). Men returned to their usual activity in 8.6 days on average (abstract only).29

Harms, and how they were counted

Side effects were mostly mild and short-lived: burning, blood in the urine, and a sudden need to pee (urgency). The trial reported no new lasting erection or ejaculation problems.29

Who was studied, and who it is for

The lift is only for prostates of the sizes tested, and not for every prostate shape. Ask whether yours fits.29

What is not known

In one large U.S. records study, repeat treatment in everyday practice ran higher than the trial's figure (see the section on repeat treatment below).30

Water vapor (steam) therapy

How well it works
Early studies suggest Eases symptoms, often within 3 months; about 1 out of 7 men need more surgery within 5 years
What it involves
Steam through the penis, often in the office
Recovery
Not stated in our sources
Sex life
Lasting erection problems rare

Steam, released through a scope in the urethra, destroys some of the inner prostate tissue. It is often done in the doctor's office.3, 31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest steam therapy eases symptoms, often within 3 months, in prostates of the size tested. In U.S. health records, about 1 out of 7 men need more surgery within 5 years.30, 32

The studies

  • In the maker's trial, fewer men needed more surgery than in everyday U.S. records. 197 men with prostates of 30 to 80 cc were assigned by chance to steam or a sham at first, then followed to 5 years. The maker paid for the trial. In the trial, 4.4% needed more surgery by 5 years. That is lower than the figure from the U.S. health-records study (abstract only).32

Harms, and how they were counted

Side effects were mostly mild and settled quickly. The trials reported no lasting new erection problems.32, 33

Who was studied, and who it is for

Steam therapy is only for prostates of the sizes tested. Ask whether yours fits.32

What is not known

How much of the gap between the trial's and the records study's repeat-treatment figures comes from the men treated, and how much from everyday practice outside trials.30, 32

Temporary implant

How well it works
Early studies suggest 78.6% improved by 3 or more symptom-score points at 3 months, against 60% after a sham (abstract only)
What it involves
A device left in the prostate for about a week
Recovery
Removed after about a week
Sex life
New problems seem uncommon

A small folded device made of nitinol (a bendable metal) is left for about a week in the part of the urethra that runs through the prostate. As it opens, it presses grooves into the tissue. Then it is removed.31, 34

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it helps some men. Many men also felt better after a sham, so how much of the relief comes from the device is unclear.34

The studies

  • More men improved than after a sham, but many improved after the sham too. 175 men were assigned by chance to the implant or a sham. The maker paid for the trial. At 3 months, 78.6% after the implant improved by at least 3 symptom-score points, against 60% after the sham. That is a low bar. Side effects (adverse events): 38.1% after the implant against 17.5% after the sham (abstract only).34
  • Longer results come from one small study with no comparison group. A later study of one group, with no comparison group, had results for 41 men at 50 to 79 months (abstract only).35

Harms, and how they were counted

Side effects were mostly mild and short-lived. However, they were more common than after the sham (see above). No new erection or ejaculation problems were reported.34

What is not known

How long it lasts: the longer data come from one small study of a single group.35

Medicine-coated balloon

How well it works
Early studies suggest Eases symptoms for at least 2 years, in the prostate sizes tested
What it involves
A coated balloon through the penis, in the office
Recovery
Not stated in our sources
Sex life
Usually unchanged

A balloon coated with a drug (paclitaxel) widens the space between the prostate's two halves (lobes). The drug is meant to stop the channel closing again.36, 38

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it eases symptoms for at least 2 years, in prostates of the size tested.36, 37

The studies

  • Symptoms eased after the balloon, but also a lot after the sham. 148 men with prostates of 20 to 80 g were assigned by chance to the balloon or a sham, 2 to 1. Neither the men nor the doctors measuring results knew which they had (double-blind). The maker paid for the trial. After the sham, symptom scores improved 8.0 points at 3 months, a large response to a fake procedure. After the balloon, they improved 11.5 points at 1 year.36
  • About two thirds of men had better symptom scores at two years, with no comparison group by then. At 2 years, 67.5% of men (56 of 83 with data) had symptom scores 30% or more better without another treatment. There was no comparison group after year 1 (abstract only).37

Harms, and how they were counted

Blood in the urine and urinary infection were the most common side effects. Sexual function was unchanged.37

What is not known

Longer results against a comparison group.37

Sex life after each treatment: how the studies counted it

Ejaculation can be measured three ways: any change (including less semen), retrograde ejaculation (semen going into the bladder), or complete loss of ejaculation. They are different outcomes and cannot be ranked against each other.39, 40

  • After PAE, studies that asked about retrograde or absent ejaculation find it uncommon.
    • A review of 19 studies of newer, less invasive treatments, counting men assigned to them by chance, looked at retrograde ejaculation (semen going into the bladder). It was under 2% by 1 year for PAE, steam, the lift, and waterjet surgery (abstract only).40
    • In 147 men in Italy who were not suitable for surgery or declined it, 130 of them (88.5%) said at 12 months that ejaculation was preserved (forward, not into the bladder). Erection scores (IIEF) rose slightly at 12 months, a change that could be due to chance, and by more than chance would explain at 18 months (abstract only).41
    • In 32 men in a row at one French centre, sent for PAE after medicines failed and surgery was refused or not possible, none had retrograde ejaculation (abstract only).42
    • In the trial against two pills in men with no earlier treatment, ejaculation changed in 1 man (5%) after PAE, against 20% of men on the pills. Lower sex drive: 40% of men on the pills.17
  • Against TURP, pooled, ejaculation problems were less likely after PAE. The Cochrane review found ejaculation problems less likely after PAE than after TURP. At 13 to 24 months, men were about two thirds as likely to have them after PAE (risk ratio 0.67; range 0.45 to 0.98; 1 randomized trial of 50 men). Up to 12 months, they were about half as likely (risk ratio 0.51; range 0.35 to 0.73; 1 study of 260 men that did not assign treatment by chance). Both are low certainty.14
  • The higher PAE figure comes from a broad definition. The Swiss trial against TURP was later re-read for ejaculation. It counted any ejaculation problem, including less semen. At 12 weeks, 56% of men after PAE had one (14 of 25 men who could be assessed), against 84% after TURP (21 of 25). Complete loss of ejaculation (anejaculation): 16% after PAE against 52% after TURP. Less semen: 40% after PAE. It was one trial, led by urologists, with one doctor doing every embolization.39
  • On erections, no study shows harm on average.
    • Against TURP, the Cochrane review found little to no difference in erection scores (IIEF-5). In the first year, the difference between PAE and TURP was -0.50 points (range -5.88 to 4.88; 2 trials, 120 men). At 13 to 24 months, it was 3.09 points (range -0.76 to 6.94; 1 trial, 81 men). Both are low certainty.14
    • In the study across several countries, erection scores (SHIM) were 13.8 before and 13.9 at 12 months, a change that could be due to chance (p = 0.912).21
    • In 551 procedures at one centre, erection scores (IIEF) stayed steady (abstract only).43
    • In 183 men at one U.S. centre, men whose urine symptoms improved more also tended to have erection scores (SHIM) improve more. This was clearest in men who had erection problems beforehand: 26.3% of the stronger responders against 3.8% of the weaker responders reached the smallest change men notice. The study found no link between urine improvement and ejaculation score (MSHQ-EjD) (abstract only).44
    • A pooled analysis (meta-analysis) of 35 studies and 2,653 men across four less invasive treatments found that only PAE improved erection scores (IIEF-5), from 6 to 12 months. These were different studies, not a direct comparison (abstract only).45
  • No study gives a rate of new erection problems large enough to quote.
  • After TURP and laser surgery, retrograde ejaculation is common: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only), by one review's count.46
  • The lift, steam, implant, and balloon trials reported no new lasting erection or ejaculation problems, each in its own maker-sponsored trial.29, 33, 34, 37

Repeat treatment, and what each figure counts

Each option's repeat-treatment figure on the short guide comes from a different kind of study. PAE's comes from a single-centre study that counts a second PAE or surgery. The lift's comes from its maker's trial, steam's from U.S. health records, and TURP's from a government page over a much longer span. They are not a ranking.3, 27, 29, 30

  • For PAE, studies without a comparison group count differently and do not agree in the long run.24, 26
    • A study of 1,072 men at one centre over 10 years, looking back at their records. Repeat treatment: 3.4% at 1 year, 21.1% at 5 years, and 58.1% at 10 years. At 5 years, that was a second PAE in 9.5% and prostate surgery in 11.6%. Going back on prostate medicine (31.3% of men) is not counted in it (abstract only).27
    • A U.S. insurance-claims study of 959 men whose first procedure for an enlarged prostate (BPH) was PAE. Any repeat treatment: 7.5% at 1 year, 17.1% at 3 years, and 25.8% at 5 years (range 16.2 to 34.3; only 23 of these men were still followed at 5 years). TURP was the most common repeat treatment, 9.1% at 5 years (abstract only).47
    • A U.S. records study of 103,019 men's first procedure for an enlarged prostate (2005 to 2025) found that 23.4% had another operation within 10 years of PAE.48
  • In randomized trials, repeat treatment was more likely after PAE than after TURP. In the Cochrane review, men were about three times as likely to need a repeat treatment after PAE as after TURP at 6 to 12 months (risk ratio 3.20; range 1.41 to 7.27; 4 trials, 303 men), about 118 against 37 per 1,000 men. At 13 to 24 months, they were nearly four times as likely (risk ratio 3.80; range 1.32 to 10.93; 1 trial, 81 men). Both results are moderate certainty.14
  • Two studies counted several options the same way, each without adjusting for which men had which.
    • One U.S. health-records study covered 420,611 procedures from 2014 to 2024. It did not adjust for which men had which procedure. More surgery at 5 years: 16% after the lift, 14% after steam, 7.1% after TURP, 4.4% after laser removal of the inner prostate (HoLEP or ThuLEP), and 1.2% after simple prostatectomy (surgery that removes the inner prostate through the belly). It gives no figure for PAE (abstract only).30
    • The other, of 103,019 men in U.S. records, gives another operation within 10 years: 6.1% after TURP, 2.4% after laser removal of the inner prostate (enucleation), 9.1% after laser vaporization (GreenLight), 0.9% after open prostatectomy, and 23.4% after PAE. Its authors note that billing codes can also count planned second-stage surgery or surgery for a complication. Prostate size and symptoms were not recorded.48

Removing more tissue leaves less to re-treat, but it means a bigger operation. A second procedure after a less invasive one is a real cost (time with symptoms, another procedure). It is counted here beside what the first one spared.14, 27

TURP vs. laser, waterjet, and open surgery

Surgery removes the part of the prostate that blocks the flow of urine.

Transurethral resection (TURP)

The surgeon removes the inside of the prostate through a scope in the penis. The man is asleep (general anesthesia) or numb from the waist down (spinal anesthesia). Most men stay in hospital and have a catheter for 1 to 3 days. Recovery takes 3 to 6 weeks.49, 52

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows TURP relieves symptoms most of the time. It is the comparison most other procedures are measured against. Over a long time, about 1 out of 10 men need more surgery within 20 years.3, 49, 50

The studies

  • The two kinds of TURP relieve symptoms about equally; bipolar TURP means fewer blood transfusions. A Cochrane review of 59 randomized trials and 8,924 men found that two kinds of TURP, bipolar and standard (monopolar), relieve symptoms about equally (moderate certainty). Men having bipolar TURP were less than half as likely to need a blood transfusion as men having standard TURP (risk ratio 0.42). They were about a sixth as likely to get TUR syndrome, a problem caused by absorbing fluid during surgery (risk ratio 0.17). Only 4 trials reported who funded them.50
  • Against PAE, TURP improved urine flow more and needed fewer repeat treatments. See the trials above.14, 15

Harms, and how they were counted

Retrograde ejaculation (semen going into the bladder, a dry orgasm) is common and can be permanent. How often depends on how it was counted: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only). In the waterjet trial, loss of ejaculation after TURP was lower (see below). Bleeding, infection, erection problems, leaking urine, and scar tissue narrowing the urethra can happen.46, 49, 51– to 53

Who was studied, and who it is for

TURP suits most prostate sizes. Very large prostates may need removal of the inner prostate (enucleation) or open surgery.31, 54

Laser surgery

How well it works

What it involves
Laser surgery through the penis
Recovery

A laser, passed through a scope, either shells out the enlarged parts of the prostate (holmium laser enucleation, HoLEP) or burns them away (vaporization). Most men go home the same day or the next, with a catheter for a few days.31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest HoLEP relieves symptoms at least as well as TURP. The newest Cochrane review rates that comparison low certainty.55, 56

The studies

  • Against TURP, HoLEP meant fewer blood transfusions and shorter hospital stays. A pooled analysis (meta-analysis) of 8 randomized trials in 764 men with prostates under 100 mL compared HoLEP with TURP. Men having HoLEP were about a sixth as likely to need a blood transfusion as men having TURP (risk ratio 0.16). They also had a catheter for less time and a shorter hospital stay. The certainty is moderate to low (abstract only).56

Harms, and how they were counted

Retrograde ejaculation is common after laser surgery too: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only). Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.31, 46

Waterjet surgery

How well it works
Early studies suggest Symptom score 15.1 points better at 5 years, against 13.2 with TURP, in one maker-sponsored trial
What it involves
Water-jet surgery through the penis
Recovery
Urine tube for a few days
Sex life
Ejaculation problems less common than after TURP

A high-pressure water jet, passed through a scope, removes the blocking tissue. A catheter stays in for a few days.31, 51

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it works about as well as TURP for at least 5 years, in small to medium prostates, from one maker-sponsored trial.51

The studies

  • Symptoms improved about as much as after TURP, with less loss of ejaculation. 181 men with prostates of 30 to 80 mL were assigned by chance to waterjet surgery or TURP. Neither the men nor the doctors measuring results knew which they had (double-blind). The maker paid for the trial. At 5 years, symptom scores had improved 15.1 points after waterjet against 13.2 after TURP. The trial tested whether waterjet was better, and the difference could be due to chance (p = 0.2764). Loss of ejaculation caused by the procedure, still present at 3 months: 7% after waterjet against 25% after TURP. No 5-year rate is given.51

Harms, and how they were counted

Ejaculation problems were less common than after TURP in the same trial. However, about 1 out of 14 men still had them in the first months. Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.31, 51

How well it works
Often used when the gland is too large for other surgery; our sources give no trial of it against TURP or laser surgery

Open or robotic surgery (simple prostatectomy) removes the inner prostate through the lower belly. After open surgery, most men stay in hospital 2 to 4 days and recover in about 6 weeks.3, 54

Bottom line

It is often used when the gland is too large for other surgery. Our sources give no trial of it against TURP or laser surgery.54

The studies

  • Against PAE in very large prostates, open surgery relieved the blockage more. See the PoPAE trial above.19

Harms, and how they were counted

Dry orgasms, leaking urine, bleeding, and infection can happen, along with the risks of any operation on the belly.54
