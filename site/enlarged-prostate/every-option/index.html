Enlarged prostate treatments compared, side by side

This page is for readers who want the studies behind the short guide. Each treatment starts with the conclusion and how sure we are, in the same certainty words the short guide uses. Then come the study sizes, designs, comparison groups, follow-up, harms and how they were counted, who was studied, funding, and what is not known.

Numbers from different studies are not head-to-head comparisons. They come from different men, follow-up times, and outcome definitions, so this page never lines them up as if they did.

A number read only in a study's abstract, not its full paper, is marked (abstract only).

Symptom scores are the IPSS, where higher is worse. Erection scores are the IIEF or SHIM, where higher is better.

Funding is named where we found it; where it is not named, it was not checked.

Every Option's physician performs prostate artery embolization. It is held to the same standard of evidence as every other option here.

BPH medicines compared

These ease symptoms like frequent peeing and a weak stream. No procedure is needed.

Alpha blockers

Tamsulosin and similar pills relax the smooth muscle of the bladder neck and prostate.1, 2

Bottom line

Good evidence suggests alpha blockers ease symptoms for many men, and most notice a change within 3 to 7 days.1, 2

The studies

  • In the label's placebo-controlled trials of tamsulosin 0.4 mg, dizziness was reported by 14.9% against 10.1% on placebo, and abnormal ejaculation by 8.4% against 0.2%.3

Harms, and how they were counted

Dizziness on standing, and a change in ejaculation (less or no semen). The label tells men planning cataract or glaucoma surgery to tell their eye surgeon, because of a floppy-iris problem during the operation.3

Who was studied, and who it is for

Usually the first medicine tried for bothersome symptoms. They treat symptoms, not the growth, so they do not lower the chance of retention or surgery on their own.2, 4

Prostate-shrinking pills

Finasteride and dutasteride block the enzyme that converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone, so the gland slowly shrinks; it takes 3 to 6 months to feel the effect.1, 6

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows they lower the chance of retention and surgery in men with enlarged prostates: Finasteride cut the chance of surgery about in half over 4 years. Adding one to an alpha blocker helps more than an alpha blocker alone in larger glands.4– to 8

The studies

  • PLESS randomized 3,040 men with enlarged prostates to finasteride or placebo for 4 years: 69 of 1,513 men on finasteride (5%) and 152 of 1,503 on placebo (10%) had surgery (abstract only).5
  • MTOPS randomized 3,047 men to placebo, doxazosin (an alpha blocker), finasteride, or both, for a mean of about 4.5 years. Clinical progression (a symptom score rise of 4 points or more, urinary retention, incontinence, kidney insufficiency or recurrent infection) was cut by 66% with the combination against placebo, and the combination and finasteride alone lowered retention and invasive treatment (abstract only).4
  • In CombAT, a drug-maker-funded trial of dutasteride plus tamsulosin, the combination eased symptoms more than either drug alone, but did not lower retention or surgery more than dutasteride alone.8

Harms, and how they were counted

Lower sex drive, erection problems and less semen. They cut the PSA level about in half, so a PSA result must be read with that in mind. Both labels warn they may raise the chance of a higher-grade prostate cancer.6, 7

Who was studied, and who it is for

The trials enrolled men with enlarged prostates.4, 5

Tadalafil

A daily low dose of tadalafil, the erection pill, relaxes smooth muscle in the bladder and prostate.2, 10

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it eases symptoms a little. The pooled effect is small and the evidence is rated low.9, 10

The studies

  • In the Cochrane review of these pills, the symptom score (IPSS) fell 1.89 points more than with placebo (95% CI 1.50 to 2.27) over 6 to 12 weeks, low-certainty evidence; adverse events were more common (risk ratio 1.42).9

Harms, and how they were counted

Headache, indigestion and back pain. It must not be taken with nitrate heart medicines, and the label does not recommend it with alpha blockers for this use.10

Who was studied, and who it is for

It may suit men who also want help with erections (our inference; it is also an erection pill).10

Is waiting safe?

For most men without retention, kidney damage or bladder stones, the evidence suggests that waiting and stepping up treatment is reasonable. A Veterans Affairs trial randomized 556 men with moderate symptoms to TURP or watchful waiting: Treatment failure in 23 against 47 men (risk ratio 0.48), and 24% of the waiting men had surgery within 3 years (abstract only). Its authors call waiting usually a safe alternative for men less bothered by symptoms. The trial enrolled men decades ago, before today's medicines and procedures.2, 11

This does not apply to men already in retention, with kidney damage, or with bladder stones; they need treatment, often surgery.2, 12

PAE vs. other less invasive treatments

These treat the prostate without major surgery. Nothing is cut out of your body. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Prostate artery embolization (PAE)

An interventional radiologist passes a catheter from the wrist or groin into the arteries that feed the prostate, under local anesthetic with light sedation, and injects small particles that cut the gland's blood supply so it shrinks. Most men do not need a urine tube. In the UK registry, 71% of PAE procedures were day cases or outpatient, while 80% of men having TURP stayed at least 1 night (abstract only).22, 25, 28

Bottom line

Early results are encouraging. Against a sham, PAE clearly eased symptoms in the short term, in one trial. Against TURP, symptom relief in the first year or two may be close, but a gap that matters has not been ruled out, TURP opens the channel more, and more men need a second procedure after PAE. Against two pills in men with larger glands, two small open trials favour PAE.13– to 17

The studies

  • Against a sham. 80 men with severe symptoms at one private clinic, run by the group that developed the technique. At 6 months the trial reports a symptom score difference of 13.2 points in favour of PAE (95% CI 10.2 to 16.2); the Cochrane review's figure for the same trial is 12.07 points (8.69 to 15.45), moderate certainty. The blinded comparison ends at 6 months, when sham patients could cross over (abstract only).13, 14
  • Against TURP, pooled. Cochrane review, searched to November 2021, mean prostate size 72.8 mL. Against TURP up to 12 months: 6 randomized trials, 360 men, symptom score difference 1.72 points in favour of TURP (95% CI -0.37 to 3.81), low certainty. At 24 months: 2 trials, 176 men, 2.58 points (-1.54 to 6.71), low certainty. Both intervals include the 3-point smallest difference men notice, so a gap that matters has not been ruled out.14
  • The best single trial against TURP. The Swiss trial randomized 103 men at one centre, open label, against TURP. At 12 weeks the adjusted symptom score gap was 2.87 points in favour of TURP (95% CI 0.53 to 5.21). Harms were counted the same way in both arms (modified Clavien grading, prospectively, with independent monitoring): 36 adverse events after PAE against 70 after TURP, and two or more events in 16.7% against 45.1%. Severe pain in the first 12 to 24 hours was more common after PAE (18.8% against 4.3%, p = 0.06).15
  • At 5 years in the same trial, only 18 of 48 PAE men and 38 of 51 TURP men were still followed. The symptom score had fallen 7.78 points after PAE and 11.57 after TURP (difference 3.79, 95% CI -0.66 to 8.24, p = 0.092), and urine flow rose 3.59 against 9.30 mL/s (abstract only).18
  • Against pills. PARTEM randomized 90 men with prostates of 50 mL or more, still bothered on an alpha blocker, to PAE or dutasteride plus tamsulosin, open label, for 9 months: The symptom score fell 10.0 points against 5.7 (difference 4.4, 95% CI 1.9 to 6.9). Funded by the French Ministry of Health, with a grant from an embolic maker named in the abstract (abstract only).16
  • P-EASY ADVANCE randomized 39 men with no earlier treatment and prostates of about 86 mL to PAE or tamsulosin plus dutasteride, open label: 63% against 28% were no longer obstructed on pressure-flow testing (p = 0.03), and the symptom score fell more after PAE (abstract only).17
  • In very large prostates. PoPAE randomized 48 men with prostates over 80 mL to PAE (23) or open simple prostatectomy (25), 6 months: Symptom scores and leftover urine fell similarly, but urine flow rose 8.3 against 15.1 mL/s, and 70% of PAE men against 12% after open surgery were still obstructed on pressure-flow testing (abstract only).19
  • Without a comparison group, the long series. 630 consecutive men at one centre, no comparison group: Clinical success by the authors' own definition was 81.9% at 1 to 3 years and 76.3% at more than 3 to 6.5 years (abstract only).20
  • An international multicentre prospective study of 478 men (405 with symptoms, 73 in retention), funded by an embolic maker: Symptom score 21.8 before and 11.2 at 24 months; 65.8% of men in retention became and stayed catheter-free; any adverse event 11.5%, serious 2.1%.21

Harms, and how they were counted

After PAE, a burning feeling in the lower belly, frequent peeing and pelvic pain for a few days are common; a little blood in the urine or stool can happen. Serious problems: The Cochrane review gives major adverse events against TURP, risk ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.19 to 2.97) up to 12 months (4 trials, 250 men) and 0.91 (0.20 to 4.05) at 13 to 24 months (2 trials, 206 men), both very low certainty. In cohorts, serious-event rates depend on who counted and how: A Danish nationwide prospective cohort of 336 men (median prostate 101 mL; 26% dependent on a urine tube): Serious adverse events 7.7%; repeat treatment 9.5% at 2 years and 18% at 5 years; 60% of the tube-dependent men were tube-free within 6 months (abstract only). The multicentre study above gives its own rate. Rare severe harms from particles reaching the bladder, rectum or penis appear mainly as case reports, with no reliable rate. How harms were counted matters: Only the Swiss trial graded both arms the same way, and older trials may have under-reported harms after TURP, so cohort rates for PAE cannot be set beside surgical series.14, 15, 21– to 24

Who was studied, and who it is for

The trials enrolled men fit for either treatment, with a mean prostate around the size in the pooled review. Men who have PAE in practice are often older, with larger glands, in retention, on blood thinners, or unwilling or unable to have surgery, as in the Danish cohort above. That cuts both ways (our inference): Sicker men raise harm and failure, while motivated, well-counselled men raise symptom response. No cohort adjusted for frailty. In retention, cohorts report most men become catheter-free: 1,075 men at one centre in the U.S., read back from records, mean follow-up 458 days, harms graded by the Society of Interventional Radiology system: 16% of men eligible for a repeat had one, up to 60 months; 94% of 126 men in retention were catheter-free at 3 months (abstract only). No randomized trial of PAE against surgery in catheter-dependent men was found.14, 21, 24– to 26

What experts say

The American Urological Association's 2026 guideline gives a conditional recommendation, evidence grade B, for PAE with particle embolics in prostates of 50 cc or more. It says symptom relief can be about as much as TURP for up to 2 years, with more repeat treatment than TURP. It advises a urologist's assessment before PAE, PAE by interventional radiologists trained in it, and offers PAE, among other options, to men on blood thinners or at high risk of bleeding. It says men should be told PAE exposes them to radiation. For men who depend on a catheter, it recommends surgery that removes tissue.12, 14

What is not known

Whether PAE spares surgery or buys years before it: No trial reports time to surgery. That PAE leaves surgery open is shown; small series of surgery after PAE (robotic, simple prostatectomy, TURP) show no signal of harm, but no good evidence either way. How it compares with laser enucleation (HoLEP): No randomized trial. Long-term results against a randomized control: The one long trial lost most PAE men to follow-up. Which particle and size work best: Not settled.14, 18, 27

Urethral lift

Small implants, placed through a scope in the urethra, pull the prostate lobes apart like curtain tiebacks. Nothing is removed.2, 31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest the lift eases symptoms, often within 2 weeks, in prostates of the size tested. In its trial, about 1 out of 7 men need another procedure within 5 years.29

The studies

  • 206 men with prostates of 30 to 80 cc, randomized against a sham, then followed without a comparison group to 5 years; sponsored by the maker. Surgical retreatment was 13.6% over 5 years, and men returned to their usual activity in a mean of 8.6 days (abstract only).29

Harms, and how they were counted

Mostly mild and short-lived: Burning, blood in the urine, urgency. The trial reported no new lasting erection or ejaculation problems.29

Who was studied, and who it is for

Only for prostates of the sizes tested, and not for every shape of gland; ask whether yours fits.29

What is not known

Real-world repeat treatment runs higher than the trial's figure in one large U.S. Records study (see the section on repeat treatment below).30

Water vapor (steam) therapy

Steam released through a scope in the urethra destroys some of the inner prostate tissue. Often done in the office.2, 31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest steam therapy eases symptoms, often within 3 months, in prostates of the size tested. In U.S. Health records, about 1 out of 7 men need more surgery within 5 years.30, 32

The studies

  • 197 men with prostates of 30 to 80 cc, randomized against a sham at first, then followed to 5 years; sponsored by the maker. Surgical retreatment in the trial was 4.4% at 5 years, a lower figure than the health-records study gives (abstract only).32

Harms, and how they were counted

Mostly mild and quickly settling. The trials reported no lasting new erection problems.32, 33

Who was studied, and who it is for

Only for prostates of the sizes tested; ask whether yours fits.32

What is not known

How much of the difference between the trial's and the records study's repeat-treatment figures comes from the men treated, and how much from practice outside trials.30, 32

Temporary implant

A folded nitinol device is left in the prostatic urethra for about a week. As it opens, it presses grooves into the tissue; then it is removed.31, 34

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it helps some men. Many men also felt better after a sham, so how much of the relief comes from the device is unclear.34

The studies

  • 175 men, randomized against a sham, sponsored by the maker: At 3 months 78.6% against 60% after the sham improved by at least 3 symptom-score points, a low bar. Adverse events were 38.1% against 17.5% (abstract only).34
  • A single-group follow-up had results for 41 men at 50 to 79 months (abstract only).35

Harms, and how they were counted

Mostly mild and short-lived; adverse events were more common than after the sham (see above). No new erection or ejaculation problems were reported.34

What is not known

How long it lasts: The longer data come from one small single-group study.35

Medicine-coated balloon

A balloon coated with paclitaxel widens the space between the prostate's two lobes; the drug is meant to stop the channel closing again.36, 38

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it eases symptoms for at least 2 years, in prostates of the size tested.36, 37

The studies

  • 148 men with prostates of 20 to 80 g, randomized 2 to 1 against a sham, double-blind, sponsored by the maker. The sham arm's symptom score fell 8.0 points at 3 months, a large sham response; the treated arm's fell 11.5 at 1 year.36
  • At 2 years, with no comparison group after year 1, 67.5% (56 of 83 with data) had improved by 30% or more without retreatment (abstract only).37

Harms, and how they were counted

Blood in the urine and urinary infection were the most common. Sexual function was unchanged.37

What is not known

Longer results against a control group.37

Sex life after each treatment: how the studies counted it

Ejaculation can be measured three ways: Any change (including less semen), retrograde ejaculation (semen going into the bladder), or complete loss of ejaculation. They are different outcomes and cannot be ranked against each other.39, 40

After PAE, studies that asked about retrograde or absent ejaculation find it uncommon. A systematic review of men randomized in 19 studies of newer less invasive treatments counted retrograde ejaculation (semen going into the bladder): Under 2% by 1 year for PAE, steam, the lift and waterjet (abstract only). In 147 men in Italy who were not suitable for or declined surgery, 130 (88.5%) said at 12 months that ejaculation was preserved (forward, not into the bladder); erection scores (IIEF) rose slightly, not significantly, at 12 months and significantly at 18 (abstract only). In 32 consecutive men at one French centre, referred after medicines failed and surgery was refused or not possible, there was no retrograde ejaculation (abstract only). In the trial against two pills in untreated men, ejaculation changed in 1 man (5%) after PAE against 20% on the pills, and 40% on the pills reported lower sex drive. Pooled against TURP, the Cochrane review found ejaculatory disorders less likely after PAE than TURP, risk ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.45 to 0.98) at 13 to 24 months in 1 randomized trial of 50 men, and 0.51 (0.35 to 0.73) up to 12 months in 1 non-randomized study of 260 men, both low certainty.14, 17, 40– to 42

The higher PAE figure comes from a broad definition. In the Swiss trial against TURP, re-read for ejaculation, any ejaculatory disorder was counted, including less semen: 56% after PAE (14 of 25 men who could be assessed) against 84% after TURP (21 of 25) at 12 weeks. Complete loss of ejaculation (anejaculation) was 16% after PAE against 52% after TURP, and less semen 40% after PAE. One urology-led trial with one embolization operator.39

On erections, no study shows harm on average. Against TURP, the Cochrane review found little to no difference in erection score, IIEF-5 difference -0.50 points (95% CI -5.88 to 4.88; 2 trials, 120 men) in the first year and 3.09 points (-0.76 to 6.94; 1 trial, 81 men) at 13 to 24 months, both low certainty. In the multicentre study, the erection score (SHIM) was 13.8 before and 13.9 at 12 months (p = 0.912). In 551 procedures at one centre, erection scores (IIEF) stayed stable (abstract only). In 183 men at one centre in the U.S., greater urinary improvement went with greater erection-score (SHIM) improvement, most in men who had erection problems beforehand (26.3% against 3.8% of weaker responders reached the smallest change men notice), and found no link between urinary improvement and ejaculation score (MSHQ-EjD) (abstract only). A meta-analysis of 35 studies and 2,653 men across four less invasive treatments found that only PAE improved the IIEF-5 erection score, from 6 to 12 months; these are different studies, not compared directly (abstract only). No study gives a rate of new erection problems large enough to quote.14, 21, 43– to 45

After TURP and laser surgery, retrograde ejaculation is common: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only), by one review's count. The lift, steam, implant and balloon trials reported no new lasting erection or ejaculation problems, each in its own sponsored trial.29, 33, 34, 37, 46

Repeat treatment, and what each figure counts

Each option's repeat-treatment figure on the short guide comes from a different kind of study: PAE from a single-centre cohort that counts a second PAE or surgery, the lift from its maker's trial, steam from U.S. Health records, and TURP from a government page over a much longer span. They are not a ranking.2, 27, 29, 30

For PAE, the cohorts count differently and do not agree in the long run: 1,072 men at one centre over 10 years, read back from records; repeat treatment 3.4% at 1 year, 21.1% at 5 years and 58.1% at 10 years. At 5 years that was a second PAE in 9.5% and prostate surgery in 11.6%. Going back on prostate medicine (31.3% of men) is not counted in it (abstract only). A U.S. Insurance-claims study of 959 men whose first BPH procedure was PAE; any repeat treatment 7.5% at 1 year, 17.1% at 3 years and 25.8% at 5 years (95% CI 16.2 to 34.3; only 23 men still followed at 5 years). TURP was the most common repeat, 9.1% at 5 years (abstract only). A U.S. Records study of 103,019 men's first BPH procedure (2005 to 2025) gives 23.4% reoperation 10 years after PAE. Against TURP in randomized trials, Repeat treatment against TURP in the Cochrane review, risk ratio 3.20 (95% CI 1.41 to 7.27) at 6 to 12 months, 4 trials, 303 men, about 118 against 37 per 1,000; and 3.80 (1.32 to 10.93) at 13 to 24 months, 1 trial, 81 men. Both moderate certainty.14, 24, 26, 27, 47, 48

Two studies counted several options the same way, each unadjusted for who had which. In one U.S. Health-records study of 420,611 procedures from 2014 to 2024 (unadjusted for who had which), surgical retreatment at 5 years was 16% after the lift, 14% after steam, 7.1% after TURP, 4.4% after laser enucleation (HoLEP or ThuLEP) and 1.2% after simple prostatectomy. It reports no PAE figure (abstract only). The second, of 103,019 men in U.S. Records, gives surgical reoperation at 10 years of 6.1% after TURP, 2.4% after laser enucleation, 9.1% after laser vaporization (GreenLight), 0.9% after open prostatectomy and 23.4% after PAE; its authors note that billing codes can catch staged or complication surgery and that gland size and symptoms were not recorded.30, 48

Removing more tissue leaves less to re-treat, but costs a bigger operation. A second procedure after a less invasive one is a real cost (time with symptoms, another procedure), counted here beside what the first one spared.14, 27

TURP vs. laser, waterjet, and open surgery

Surgery removes the part of the prostate that blocks the flow of urine.

Transurethral resection (TURP)

The surgeon removes the inside of the prostate through a scope in the penis, under general or spinal anesthesia. Most men stay in hospital and have a catheter for 1 to 3 days; recovery takes 3 to 6 weeks.49, 52

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows TURP relieves symptoms most of the time. It is the comparison most other procedures are measured against. Over a long time, about 1 out of 10 men need more surgery within 20 years.2, 49, 50

The studies

  • A Cochrane review of 59 randomized trials and 8,924 men found bipolar and standard (monopolar) TURP relieve symptoms about equally (moderate certainty); bipolar lowered transfusion (risk ratio 0.42) and TUR syndrome, a fluid-absorption problem (risk ratio 0.17). Only 4 trials reported funding.50
  • Against PAE, see the trials above: TURP improved flow and needed fewer repeats.14, 15

Harms, and how they were counted

Retrograde ejaculation (dry orgasm) is common and can be permanent; how often depends on how it was counted: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only). In the waterjet trial, loss of ejaculation after TURP was lower (see below). Bleeding, infection, erection problems, leaking urine, and scar tissue narrowing the urethra can happen.46, 49, 51– to 53

Who was studied, and who it is for

For most gland sizes; very large glands may need enucleation or open surgery.31, 54

Laser surgery

A laser through a scope either shells out the enlarged lobes (holmium enucleation, HoLEP) or vaporizes them. Most men go home the same day or the next, with a catheter for a few days.31

Bottom line

Early studies suggest HoLEP relieves symptoms at least as well as TURP. The newest Cochrane review rates that comparison low certainty.55, 56

The studies

  • A meta-analysis of 8 randomized trials, 764 men with prostates under 100 mL: Fewer transfusions after HoLEP (risk ratio 0.16), and shorter catheter time and hospital stay; moderate to low certainty (abstract only).56

Harms, and how they were counted

Retrograde ejaculation is common after laser surgery too: 42% to 75% of men have retrograde ejaculation after standard TURP or laser surgery (abstract only). Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.31, 46

Waterjet surgery

A high-pressure water jet through a scope removes the blocking tissue; a catheter stays in for a few days.31, 51

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it works about as well as TURP for at least 5 years, in small to medium prostates, from one maker-sponsored trial.51

The studies

  • 181 men with prostates of 30 to 80 mL, double-blind, against TURP, sponsored by the maker. At 5 years the symptom score had improved 15.1 points against 13.2 (p = 0.2764), a test for superiority that found no difference. Procedure-related ejaculatory dysfunction (in the full text, loss of ejaculation counted as a lasting event at 3 months) was 7% against 25%; no 5-year rate is given.51

Harms, and how they were counted

Ejaculation problems were less common than after TURP in the same trial, but about 1 out of 14 men still had it in the first months. Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.31, 51

Open or robotic prostate removal

Open or robotic simple prostatectomy removes the inner gland through the lower belly. After open surgery, most men stay in hospital 2 to 4 days and recover in about 6 weeks.2, 54

Bottom line

It is often used when the gland is too large for other surgery. Our sources give no trial of it against TURP or laser surgery.54

The studies

  • Against PAE in very large glands, see the PoPAE trial above: Open surgery relieved obstruction more.19

Harms, and how they were counted

Dry orgasms, leaking urine, bleeding and infection can happen, with the risks of an abdominal operation.54
