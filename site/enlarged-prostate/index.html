What is an enlarged prostate?

The prostate is a small gland that wraps around the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the body. In almost all men, it grows with age. It can squeeze the urethra and make it hard to empty the bladder. As men get older, this gets more common, with more than 9 out of 10 men having it in their 80s.1, 2

Common symptoms1, 2

  • Frequent peeing
  • A strong, sudden urge to go
  • Getting up at night to pee
  • A weak or slow stream
  • Straining
  • Dribbling at the end
When to see a doctor

See a doctor if your bladder does not feel empty after you pee, or if self-care has not helped after 2 months. A doctor also checks for other causes, such as infection or cancer.1, 2

What is prostate artery embolization (PAE)?

Prostate artery embolization (PAE) treats an enlarged prostate without surgery. A thin tube goes through a small puncture in the wrist or groin. Tiny beads slow blood flow to the prostate so it shrinks, and urine can flow more easily. Nothing is cut out. Most men go home the same day.3, 4, 8

How it works

You stay awake, with medicine to help you relax. Most men do not need a urine tube through the penis. Most men are back to normal activities within a few days. Avoid heavy activity for about a week. After TURP surgery, recovery takes 3 to 6 weeks.3– to 7

How well does it work?

Early results are encouraging. Most men feel much better, and for many the relief lasts at least three years. The American Urological Association's 2026 guideline lists PAE as an option for men with larger prostates. It says PAE can improve symptoms by about as much as TURP surgery, for up to 2 years. It is also an option for men who take blood thinners or have a high risk of bleeding.
Some men need another treatment later, such as a second embolization or surgery: about 1 out of 5 within five years. Early studies suggest this is more likely than after surgery. If PAE works for you, it may help you avoid or put off surgery, but we don't know that yet. If it does not, surgery is still an option.9– to 14

Side effects

You may have a burning feeling in your lower belly, need to pee often, and some pelvic pain for a few days. A little blood in your urine or stool can happen. Most men keep normal ejaculation. Some notice less semen. Losing ejaculation completely is uncommon, and much less common than after TURP surgery. PAE does not seem to affect erections. Serious problems are uncommon.3, 10, 12, 15– to 19

Results vary from person to person. Talk to a doctor about whether this is right for you.

Does insurance cover prostate embolization?

An enlarged prostate is common as men age. This is not cancer and does not raise your risk of prostate cancer. Many men have no symptoms. If you have trouble peeing, embolization is one of many options. You can also consider medicines, other less invasive treatments, or surgery. The right choice depends on your symptoms and goals.1, 2

If you cannot pee at all, have a fever with chills, see a lot of blood or clots in your urine, have severe pain, or feel very sick or faint, call 911.

Your other enlarged prostate treatment options

Medicines

These ease symptoms like frequent peeing and a weak stream. No procedure is needed.

Alpha blockers

These include pills (such as tamsulosin). They relax the muscles around the prostate and bladder so urine flows more easily.1, 2

How invasive is it?
These are pills. No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests these pills ease symptoms for many men. Most men notice a change within 3 to 7 days.1, 2
Side effects
You may feel dizzy, mostly when you stand up. Ejaculation may change. If you plan eye surgery for cataracts, tell your eye doctor if you take one or have taken one.20
Prostate-shrinking pills

These are pills (such as finasteride or dutasteride). They lower a hormone that makes the prostate grow, so it slowly shrinks.1, 2, 21

How invasive is it?
These are pills. No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
It can take 3 to 6 months to feel better. Strong evidence shows they lower the chance of needing surgery. Finasteride cut that chance about in half over 4 years. Taking one with an alpha blocker may work better than an alpha blocker alone for men with larger prostates.1, 2, 21– to 24
Side effects
You may have a lower sex drive or erection problems. They cut your PSA level about in half, so tell your doctor before a PSA test. They may raise the chance of a more serious prostate cancer.1, 21, 22
Tadalafil

This is a pill. It relaxes muscles around the bladder and prostate. It is also used for erection problems.2, 25

How invasive is it?
This is a pill. No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it eases symptoms a little.2, 25, 26
Side effects
You may have a headache, upset stomach, or back pain. Do not take it with nitrate heart medicines. It is not recommended with alpha blockers for this use.25
Less invasive treatments

These treat the prostate without major surgery. Nothing is cut out of your body. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Prostate artery embolization (PAE)Details above
Urethral lift

Small implants lift and hold the prostate away from the urethra, like tiebacks on a curtain.2, 27

How invasive is it?
It is done through the penis, with no cuts in the skin. Most men need little or no urine tube time and return to normal activity in about 1 week.27, 28
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it eases symptoms, often within 2 weeks. It works only for some prostate sizes, so ask if yours fits. Some men need another procedure: about 1 out of 7 within 5 years.28
Side effects
Side effects are mostly mild and short-lived. Erections and ejaculation usually stay the same.28
Water vapor (steam) therapy

Steam released into the prostate burns away some of the inner tissue. This makes the path for urine larger.2, 27

How invasive is it?
It is done through the penis, with no cuts in the skin. It can be done in the doctor's office.27
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it eases symptoms, often within 3 months. It works only for some prostate sizes, so ask if yours fits. About 1 out of 7 men need more surgery within 5 years.29, 30
Side effects
Side effects are mostly mild and go away quickly. Lasting erection problems are rare.29, 31
Temporary implant

A small folded device is placed in the prostate for about a week. As it opens, it makes small cuts that widen the passage. Then it is removed.27, 32

How invasive is it?
It is placed and later removed through the penis, with no cuts in the skin.27, 32
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it helps some men. Many men also felt better after a fake procedure, so doctors are still learning how much of the relief comes from the implant. We do not yet know how long it lasts.32, 33
Side effects
Side effects are mostly mild and short-lived. New erection or ejaculation problems seem uncommon.32
Medicine-coated balloon

A balloon passed through the penis widens the space where the prostate squeezes the urethra. The balloon is coated with a medicine that helps keep it from closing again.36

How invasive is it?
It is done through the penis, with no cuts in the skin. It can be done in the office.34
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it eases symptoms for at least 2 years. It works only for some prostate sizes, so ask if yours fits.34, 35
Side effects
You may have blood in your urine or a bladder infection. Sex life usually stays the same.35
Surgery

Surgery removes the part of the prostate that blocks the flow of urine.

Transurethral resection (TURP)

Surgery removes the inside of the prostate with a small tool passed through the penis.37

How invasive is it?
There are no cuts in the skin. You are asleep or numb from the waist down. Most men stay in the hospital for 1 to 3 days and have a urine tube for 1 to 3 days. Recovery takes about 3 to 6 weeks.7, 37
How well does it work?
Strong evidence shows it relieves symptoms most of the time. About 1 out of 10 men need more surgery within 20 years.2, 37
Side effects
Many men have dry orgasms afterward, where semen goes into the bladder. This can be permanent. Bleeding, infection, erection problems, and leaking urine can happen. Scar tissue can narrow the urethra.2, 7, 37– to 39
Laser surgery

A laser passed through the penis removes or vaporizes the blocking tissue. One type is called HoLEP.27

How invasive is it?
There are no cuts in the skin. Most men go home the same day or the next day, with a urine tube for a few days. There is less bleeding and a shorter hospital stay than with TURP.27, 40
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest HoLEP works at least as well as TURP. It improves the flow of urine.27, 40, 41
Side effects
Many men have dry orgasms afterward. Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.27, 38
Waterjet surgery

High-pressure jets of water, passed through the penis, remove the blocking tissue.27

How invasive is it?
There are no cuts in the skin. You have a urine tube for a few days.27
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it works about as well as TURP for at least 5 years, in small to medium prostates.42
Side effects
Ejaculation problems are less common than after TURP, but about 1 out of 14 men still have them in the first months. Erection problems and leaking urine can happen.27, 42
Open or robotic prostate removal

Surgery through the belly removes the inside of a very large prostate.43

How invasive is it?
It takes a cut in the lower belly, or small cuts with a robot. After open surgery, most men stay in the hospital for 2 to 4 days with a urine tube. Recovery takes about 6 weeks.2, 43
How well does it work?
It is often used when the prostate is too large for other surgery. Most men can pee as usual afterward.43
Side effects
Dry orgasms, leaking urine, bleeding, and infection can happen.43

What should I ask my doctor?

You can ask about any option on this page, even one your doctor does not offer.

  • Which options could work for me, and which could not?
  • How would each option affect my erections and ejaculation?
  • How long would I need to recover, and will I need a urine tube?
  • Do you offer prostate artery embolization, or can you refer me to someone who does?
Common questions

Do I need treatment?

Not always. If your symptoms are mild and do not bother you, your doctor may watch them with yearly checkups. You can also drink less before bed and cut back on alcohol and caffeine. These changes can help, and they're safe to try.2

Is an enlarged prostate cancer?

No. It is not cancer, and it does not raise your risk of prostate cancer. Some symptoms can look alike, so your doctor may check your PSA, a blood test, to screen you for prostate cancer.1, 2

Will treatment affect my sex life?

It depends on the treatment. Many surgeries cause dry orgasms, where semen goes into the bladder. Some pills can lower your sex drive or cause erection problems. Ask your doctor how each option fits your goals before you choose.21, 38

Do I need surgery for an enlarged prostate?

Not always. Medicines and less invasive treatments, such as PAE (prostate artery embolization), are also options. Surgery is often used when other treatments have not helped or problems develop.2
