What insurers' policies say about embolization

We checked what Medicare and 9 large U.S. insurers publish about four embolization procedures: for fibroids (UFE), enlarged prostate (PAE), knee arthritis (GAE), and hemorrhoids (HAE). Coverage often has conditions, and it differs by procedure. Knee and hemorrhoid embolization are covered least often. Your plan may differ.

Your plan may differ. Employer self-funded plans and Medicare Advantage plans can set their own rules. Check with your plan.

Education, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about your own care. We cannot advise you on your own claim or prior authorization.

Changes coming soon

  • UnitedHealthcare: a new policy takes effect December 1, 2026. It lists knee (GAE) and hemorrhoid (HAE) embolization as unproven. On the same date, UnitedHealthcare adds an approval step for one embolization billing code on most of its Medicare Advantage plans. UnitedHealthcare notice, read November 8, 2026
  • Anthem: the Carelon guideline that Anthem plans appear to use has a new version that takes effect November 15, 2026. Our Anthem entries use the new version. The earlier version applies until then.

Decided case by caseNo specific Medicare policy; decided case by case.

Cigna

Covered with conditionsGrade 1 to 3 hemorrhoids after failed non-surgical treatment, including diet and lifestyle changes, plus one of these.

A repeat is allowed for recurrent daily bleeding. Applies where EviCore manages the service for Cigna.

  • Repeated bleeding confirmed on anoscopy (an exam inside the anus), or no other source of bleeding
  • Other symptoms in people who cannot have office procedures or surgery (blood thinners, a bleeding disorder, a weak immune system, or inflammatory bowel disease)
  • Other symptoms after failed banding or sclerotherapy (two office treatments for hemorrhoids)

Checked November 7, 2026 · Cigna Peripheral Vascular Intervention Guidelines (EviCore, V2.0.2026) · In effect November 5, 2026

Questions to ask your plan

  • Does my plan cover this procedure, and under what conditions?
  • Which written policy applies to my plan?
  • Do I need approval first (prior authorization)?
  • What will I pay out of pocket?

How we checked

Where an insurer's written policy could be read, we read the newest version we could find. For Medicare, we read the national decision and checked local decisions and articles by title.

Some policies and lists could not be opened or read. Those entries say so, and some say "Not yet checked."

We wrote each entry in our own words and link to the policy, so you can check it yourself. Each entry shows the date we checked it. Every check was made on November 7 or 8, 2026.

Where we found no policy, we say so, and we say what we searched. Finding no policy does not mean a procedure is covered.

Not included: Medicaid, and Blue Cross Blue Shield companies other than Anthem and HCSC.
