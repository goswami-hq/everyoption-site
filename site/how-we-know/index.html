How we know

We look through public information so you can find who near you does a procedure. Doctors can claim their own listing for free, and anyone can report an error.

What the two groups mean

Confirmed by physician
The doctor told us they perform it, and we have confirmed the account is theirs.
Likely performs this procedure
Public information points to this doctor performing it, or the doctor told us so from an account we have not yet confirmed (the card says so). Call the office to confirm.

Where the information comes from

  • Doctors’ profiles on hospital, university, and practice websites, and search results for those pages.
  • Practice websites.
  • Public Medicare billing records.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov, the U.S. registry of clinical studies.
  • Other websites that copy a doctor’s profile.

Cities come from the doctor’s newest page. When we only have a city from public records, the card says “City from public records; confirm location with the practice.” Distances start from the center of the ZIP code you type; we never ask where you live. When a doctor who confirmed their listing gives an office address, we measure to that office, and a doctor with several offices is shown at the closest one, with all of their offices listed on the card. Otherwise we measure to the center of the doctor’s ZIP code or city.

A blank is not a no

If a doctor is missing, it usually means we have not found them yet. It does not mean they don’t perform the procedure.

When few doctors are near you

If we find fewer than five doctors for a procedure near you, we also show Interventional radiologists nearby: doctors whose own page or practice page shows they are interventional radiologists, but where we could not confirm this procedure. Call and ask whether they perform it. Doctors whose practice website names the procedure come first, then the rest by distance. We list only those with a phone number or a web page, and never from billing records alone.

If that is still not enough, we add the nearest doctors farther away, up to 250 miles, with the distance on each card. If we know of no one within 250 miles, ask your doctor to refer you to an interventional radiologist.

Any doctor on any card can claim their listing, for free.

The badge under a name

Interventional radiologist: their own page or their practice’s page says so, or, when no page does, public Medicare billing records show they perform interventional radiology procedures. When we cannot tell, the doctor is not listed.

Who runs this

Every Option is run by a practicing interventional radiologist. Listings are free, and no one can pay to be listed, ranked higher, or sent patients.

Confirming is free. Within each group, the order is by distance only, except that interventional radiologists whose practice website names the procedure come first in their group.

Report an error

If something on a listing is wrong, anyone can tell us with the report form or by email to errors@everyoptionmd.com. A report does not change a listing by itself.

Claim your profile

Only the physician can claim their own profile, with a free account. A physician with an account can add their procedures, offices, phone, and website at once; their card stays under “Likely performs” until we know the account is theirs, and then moves to “Confirmed by physician.”
