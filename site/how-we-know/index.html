How we know

We look through public information so you can find who near you does a procedure. Doctors can confirm or correct their own listing for free.

What the two groups mean

Performs this procedure, confirmed by physician
The doctor told us they perform it. A person checks each submission before it appears.
Likely performs this procedure
Public information points to this doctor performing it. We have not heard from the doctor, so call the office to confirm.

Where the information comes from

  • Doctors’ profiles on hospital, university, and practice websites, and search results for those pages.
  • Practice websites.
  • Public Medicare billing records.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov, the U.S. registry of clinical studies.
  • Other websites that copy a doctor’s profile.

Cities come from the doctor’s newest page. When we only have a city from public records, the card says “City from public records; confirm location with the practice.” Distances are measured between ZIP code centers, not street addresses.

A blank is not a no

If a doctor is missing, it usually means we have not found them yet. It does not mean they don’t perform the procedure.

The badge under a name

Interventional radiologist: their own page or their practice’s page says so, or, when no page does, public Medicare billing records show they perform interventional radiology procedures. When we cannot tell, the doctor is not listed.

Who runs this

Every Option is run by a practicing interventional radiologist. Listings are free, and no one can pay to be listed, ranked higher, or sent patients.

Confirming is free. Within each group, the order is by distance only.

Report an error

If something is wrong, or you are a listed doctor and want to confirm or correct your listing, use the correction form or email errors@everyoptionmd.com. A person reviews every report before anything changes.
