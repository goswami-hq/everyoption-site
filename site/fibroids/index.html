What are my options for fibroids?

Fibroids are growths in the uterus (womb). They are not cancer. Many cause no problems and need no care.1 If they cause heavy periods, pain or pressure, you have many choices. Some are pills. Some are procedures that keep the uterus. Surgery is one choice too.1 The best one depends on you.

What are fibroids?

Fibroids are lumps made of muscle. They grow in or on the wall of the uterus.2 They are very common. By age 50, between 2 and 8 in 10 women have them.2 In one large study, more than 8 in 10 Black women and about 7 in 10 white women had them by 50.3

Fibroids can be one or many. They can be small or big. Some grow inside, some in the wall, and some on the outside.1 Where they are helps decide which choices fit.

Fibroids often shrink after menopause, when periods stop for good.12

What are the signs? When should I see a doctor?

Many people with fibroids feel nothing.1 When there are signs, they can be:

  • Heavy periods, sometimes with clots, or periods that last longer.1
  • Bleeding between periods.1
  • Feeling tired from losing blood. This is called anemia, which means a low blood count.2
  • Pain or cramps, or pressure or fullness low in the belly.1
  • Needing to pee a lot, or trouble with poop.12
  • Pain during sex.1

See a doctor if you have any of these signs. Also call if you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for 2 to 3 hours, or if bleeding makes you weak or lightheaded.4

When should I get help right away?

Call 911 if you pass out.5

Call your doctor now or get care right away if:5

  • You have very heavy bleeding.5
  • You feel dizzy or lightheaded, or feel like you might faint.5
  • You have new or worse pain in your belly or pelvis.5

If you are pregnant or might be and you are bleeding, call your doctor.4

What are all the options?

They start with the gentlest. Not every choice is right for every person. Each one answers the same eight questions. Some use hormones, the body’s chemical messengers.

  • Watching and waiting
  • Medicines without hormones
  • Hormone medicines
  • Hormone IUD
  • Focused ultrasound
  • Fibroid embolization
  • Radiofrequency ablation
  • Removal through the cervix
  • Endometrial ablation
  • Myomectomy

Watching and waiting

What it is
You do not treat the fibroids now. You keep an eye on them.1
How it works
Your doctor checks them with exams or an ultrasound, a scan that uses sound waves.1
Good for
Fibroids that cause no problems, or mild signs you can live with.2
Not for
Heavy bleeding, low blood count or pain that is hard to live with.
Recovery
None. There is nothing to recover from.
Could I still get pregnant?
Yes. Watching does not change this. Big fibroids can sometimes cause problems in pregnancy.6
Can fibroids come back?
They stay. They may grow, shrink or stay the same.1 They often shrink after menopause.1
Main risks
Signs can get worse over time. Go back to your doctor if they do.

Medicines without hormones

What it is
Pain pills, iron pills, and a pill called tranexamic acid. It helps blood clot so periods are lighter.17
How it works
Pain pills ease cramps. Iron builds back your blood.2 Tranexamic acid is taken only on period days, up to 5 days each month.7
Good for
Heavy periods or cramps, if you want to skip hormones.1
Not for
Tranexamic acid is not for people who take birth control with estrogen, or who have had blood clots.7
Recovery
None.
Could I still get pregnant?
Yes. These are not birth control.
Can fibroids come back?
These treat the signs, not the fibroids. Signs come back if you stop.
Main risks
Tranexamic acid can rarely cause blood clots.7 Pain pills can upset your stomach.

Hormone medicines

What it is
Birth control pills or shots. Daily pills that lower hormones. Shots for up to 3 months before surgery, to stop bleeding and build back blood.18
How it works
These change hormones so periods are lighter. Some shrink fibroids for a while.1
Good for
Heavy periods. With the daily hormone-lowering pills, about 7 in 10 bled much less. On a fake pill, 1 or 2 in 10 did.9
Not for
People who have had blood clots or some other health problems.1011
Recovery
None.
Could I still get pregnant?
Birth control pills and shots prevent it. The others are not birth control.108
Can fibroids come back?
Signs often return when you stop. Fibroids can regrow fast.2
Main risks
Hot flashes. Some weaken bones, so use is limited: up to 2 years for the daily pills, a few months for shots.10118

Hormone IUD

What it is
An IUD is a small T-shaped device placed in the uterus. This kind gives off a small amount of hormone.12
How it works
The hormone thins the lining of the uterus, so periods are lighter.2
Good for
Heavy periods, if you also want birth control. It can be used for this for up to 5 years.12
Not for
Fibroids that change the shape of the inside of the uterus.12
Recovery
It is placed in a short office visit.
Could I still get pregnant?
Not while it is in. It is birth control. You can try once it is taken out.12
Can fibroids come back?
It does not remove fibroids. It treats the bleeding.
Main risks
Spotting at first. With fibroids, it is more likely to slip out.13

Focused ultrasound

What it is
Sound waves heat and destroy fibroids. There is no cut. An MRI scanner, a large machine that takes pictures, guides it.14
How it works
Beams of sound meet at the fibroid and heat it. The fibroid gets smaller over time.14
Good for
Some fibroids that the beams can reach, in a uterus that is not too big.15
Not for
Pregnancy, many or very large fibroids, or many scars on the belly.14
Recovery
Short. Most people go back to normal soon.14
Could I still get pregnant?
Some people have had babies after it. Doctors are still studying this.14
Can fibroids come back?
Yes. Some people need more treatment if signs return.14
Main risks
Belly pain, skin burns or bruises that heal in a week or two.14 Not all insurance plans pay for it.1416

Fibroid embolization

Also called UFE or uterine artery embolization. Embolization means blocking blood flow on purpose.

What it is
No surgery, only a tiny skin nick.17
How it works
A thin tube goes in at the wrist or groin. Tiny beads block blood to the fibroids, so they shrink.171
Good for
Fibroids causing bleeding, pain or pressure, if you want to keep your uterus.18
Not for
Pregnancy, pelvic infection, or possible cancer.14
Recovery
Most go home the same day,17 back to normal in 7 to 10 days.19
Could I still get pregnant?
Maybe. Pregnancies happen, but proof is weaker than after fibroid surgery. Experts disagree.192018
Can fibroids come back?
About 1 in 3 needed more treatment within 5 years.21
Main risks
Cramps, upset stomach, low fever for 2 to 7 days.17 2 to 3 in 100 pass bits of fibroid. 1 to 5 in 100 reach menopause early, more often over 45. Some later need the uterus removed.14

Radiofrequency ablation

What it is
Heat from a small needle destroys the fibroid. Ablation means destroying tissue.1
How it works
The needle goes into the fibroid through small cuts in the belly, or through the vagina with no cuts. Ultrasound guides it.122
Good for
Some fibroids, if you want to keep your uterus.13
Not for
Ask your doctor. Not every fibroid can be reached this way.
Recovery
Most go home the same day.23
Could I still get pregnant?
Maybe. There is not much proof yet.13
Can fibroids come back?
Studies differ. In one, about 1 in 11 needed surgery within 3 years.22 In another, about 2 in 5 needed surgery within 2 years.24
Main risks
Pain or cramps. Serious problems were rare in studies.2224

Removal through the cervix

Also called hysteroscopic myomectomy. A myomectomy is surgery that takes out the fibroids.

What it is
A doctor removes fibroids from inside the uterus. There are no cuts.125
How it works
A thin camera goes in through the cervix, the opening at the bottom of the uterus. Small tools take out the fibroid.25
Good for
Fibroids that grow inside the uterus.1
Not for
Fibroids in the wall or on the outside. Pregnancy or a pelvic infection.25
Recovery
A few days.26
Could I still get pregnant?
Yes. It can be a good choice if you want children.1826
Can fibroids come back?
New fibroids can grow later.2
Main risks
Cramps and light bleeding for a few days. Heavy bleeding, infection or a tear in the uterus are rare.25

Endometrial ablation

What it is
A procedure that destroys the lining of the uterus to make periods lighter. The lining is called the endometrium.27
How it works
Tools go in through the vagina with no cuts. Heat or cold scars the lining.27
Good for
Heavy bleeding with small fibroids. It often stops periods.1 Experts disagree on using it for fibroids.18
Not for
Anyone who might want to get pregnant later.2728
Recovery
Most go home the same day and are back to normal in 1 to 2 days.27
Could I still get pregnant?
No. Pregnancy after it can be dangerous. You still need birth control.2827
Can fibroids come back?
It does not remove fibroids. It treats the lining.
Main risks
Cramps and discharge for a while. A hole in the uterus or infection are rare.27

Myomectomy: surgery to remove fibroids

What it is
Surgery that takes out the fibroids and leaves the uterus.1
How it works
A surgeon cuts out each fibroid. This is done through a larger cut in the belly or a few small cuts.26
Good for
Fibroids that cause problems, if you want children later.118
Not for
Ask your surgeon. It depends on how many fibroids you have and where they are.
Recovery
About 2 to 4 weeks with small cuts. Up to 6 weeks with a larger cut.26
Could I still get pregnant?
Yes. You may need a C-section.26 A tear in the uterus in pregnancy is rare: about 3 to 6 in 1,000.2930
Can fibroids come back?
New fibroids can grow.1 More likely if you are under 40 with many.26
Main risks
Bleeding, infection, blood clots and harm to nearby organs. These are not common.26

Back to the comparison

Hysterectomy: surgery to remove the uterus

What it is
Surgery that takes out the whole uterus.31
How it works
The uterus comes out through the vagina, through small cuts, or through a larger cut in the belly.31
Good for
Bad signs, if you do not want children, or other choices have not worked.12
Not for
Anyone who wants to get pregnant later.26
Recovery
About 2 to 4 weeks with small cuts. About 4 to 6 weeks with a larger cut.31
Could I still get pregnant?
No. Periods stop too.26
Can fibroids come back?
No. It is the only treatment that makes sure fibroids cannot come back.2
Main risks
Bleeding, infection, blood clots and harm to nearby organs. If the ovaries, which make eggs and hormones, are also taken out, menopause starts.31

How do I choose?

There is no one best choice. Your doctor will think about:21

  • How bad your signs are.
  • Whether you might want a baby later.
  • How big your fibroids are, how many you have, and where they are.
  • Your age, and how close you are to menopause.
  • Your health, and what matters most to you, like keeping your uterus or getting back to work fast.

It is OK to ask about every choice on this page, and to get a second view from a different kind of doctor.

What should I ask my doctor?

  • Where are my fibroids, and how big are they?
  • Which choices on this list could work for me? Which could not, and why?
  • Do I need treatment now, or can we wait?
  • Which choices keep my uterus?
  • How would each choice affect a future pregnancy?
  • How long would I need off work?
  • How likely are the fibroids or my signs to come back?
  • Do you also do embolization, or can you send me to someone who does?

Common questions

Do fibroids go away on their own?

Sometimes. Many do not grow and need no care. They often shrink after menopause, when periods stop for good.12

Are fibroids cancer?

No. Fibroids are not cancer. Rarely, a growth that looks like a fibroid is a hidden cancer. Estimates differ: less than 1 in 1,000,2 or about 1 in 225 to 1 in 580 people who have fibroid surgery.32

Can I get pregnant if I have fibroids?

Yes, most people can. Big fibroids can sometimes cause problems in pregnancy. Tell your doctor if you want a baby later.6

Do I need a hysterectomy for fibroids?

Not always. Many people never need one. There are pills, procedures that keep the uterus, and surgery that removes only the fibroids. A hysterectomy is the only treatment that makes sure fibroids cannot come back.12

What is the least invasive treatment for fibroids?

Watching and waiting, medicines and a hormone IUD need no procedure. Some procedures need no cuts or only a tiny nick: focused ultrasound, embolization, removal through the cervix, endometrial ablation, and one kind of radiofrequency ablation. Each fits some people and not others.11417

Who does fibroid embolization?

An interventional radiologist. This is a doctor who treats problems through tiny tubes, using X-rays to see.17 You can find one near you.

How long does it take to recover?

It depends on the choice. Medicines need no recovery. Most people are back to normal 7 to 10 days after embolization.19 Surgery can take 2 to 6 weeks.2631

Where can I find a doctor?

A gynecologist (a doctor for women’s health) can talk with you about pills and surgery. An interventional radiologist does fibroid embolization. This is a doctor who treats problems through tiny tubes, using X-rays to see.17
