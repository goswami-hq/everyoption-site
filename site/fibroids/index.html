Shrink fibroids without surgery

Fibroid embolization works through a tiny skin nick. Your uterus stays in place. Most people go home the same day.
Recovery takes about 1 week instead of 2 to 6 weeks after major surgery. How it works

Fibroids are non-cancerous growths in the uterus. Many cause no symptoms and need no treatment. When they cause heavy periods, pain, or pressure, your options fall into three groups: medicines, less invasive treatments, and surgery. The right choice depends on your symptoms and goals.1

If you have severe bleeding, severe pain, a fever, or feel very sick or faint, call 911.

Your options

Medicines

Treat symptoms like heavy bleeding and pain. No procedure needed.

Medicines without hormones

Tranexamic acid, taken only on period days, plus pain relievers and iron.123

How invasive is it?
Not invasive. These are pills.
How well does it work?
They ease bleeding and cramps but do not treat the fibroids, so symptoms return when you stop.12
Side effects
Tranexamic acid can rarely cause blood clots.2
Hormone medicines

Birth control pills or shots, daily pills that lower hormones, and short-term shots before surgery.17

How invasive is it?
Not invasive. These are pills or shots.
How well does it work?
Daily hormone-lowering pills greatly reduced bleeding for about 7 in 10 people. Symptoms often return after stopping.43
Side effects
Hot flashes. Some weaken bones, so use is limited. Not for people who have had blood clots.567
Hormone IUD

A small device placed in the uterus that thins the lining, for up to 5 years.39

How invasive is it?
Minimal. Placed during an office visit.
How well does it work?
Lightens periods but does not treat the fibroids.3
Side effects
Spotting at first. It is more likely to slip out when fibroids are present.8
Less Invasive Treatments

Treat fibroids or bleeding without major surgery, with your uterus left in place. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Fibroid embolization (UFE)

Tiny beads block blood flow to the fibroids so they shrink. You keep your uterus.10111

How invasive is it?
A tiny skin nick, with no cut in the belly, so no belly scar where a hernia can form. Most people go home the same day and recover in about a week, instead of 2 to 6 weeks after major surgery.10111213
How well does it work?
Symptoms improve for about 8 to 9 in 10 women, and 7 in 10 still have good control at 5 years. Satisfaction is about the same as surgery. About 1 in 5 to 1 in 3 have another procedure within 5 years. Over 10 years, about 2 in 3 avoid a hysterectomy.141516171819
Side effects
Cramps, nausea, and low fever for up to a week. Early menopause in 1 to 5 in 100. Serious problems are no more common than after surgery, but smaller problems are.10201621

Radiofrequency ablation

Heat from a small needle destroys the fibroid.1

How invasive is it?
Small cuts in the belly, or through the vagina with no cuts. Most people go home the same day.2223
How well does it work?
Results vary: about 1 in 11 needed surgery within 3 years in one study, and about 2 in 5 within 2 years in another.2224
Side effects
Pain and cramps. Serious problems were rare in studies.2224
Focused ultrasound

Beams of sound, guided by an MRI scanner, heat and shrink the fibroid.20

How invasive is it?
No cut. Most people recover quickly.20
How well does it work?
Fits only some fibroids, and some people need more treatment later. Not all insurance covers it.202526
Side effects
Belly pain and skin burns or bruises that usually heal in a week or two.20
Endometrial ablation

Heat or cold destroys the lining of the uterus to lighten periods.27

How invasive is it?
No cuts. Most people go home the same day and recover in 1 to 2 days.27
How well does it work?
Treats the lining, not the fibroids, which stay in place. A radiology group rates it usually not appropriate for fibroids. In heavy-bleeding studies, about 1 in 8 had a hysterectomy within 5 years.12829
Side effects
Cramps and discharge for a while. Not for anyone who may want a future pregnancy.2730
Surgery

Removes the fibroids or the whole uterus.

Removal through the cervix

A thin camera and small tools remove fibroids growing inside the uterus.131

How invasive is it?
No cuts. Recovery takes a few days.12
How well does it work?
Works only for fibroids inside the uterus. New fibroids can form later.13
Side effects
Cramps and light bleeding for a few days. Heavy bleeding, infection, or a tear in the uterus are rare.31
Myomectomy

Surgery that removes the fibroids and leaves the uterus.1

How invasive is it?
A larger cut in the belly or a few small cuts. Recovery takes about 2 to 6 weeks.12
How well does it work?
Removes the fibroids you have, but new ones can grow. Quality of life improves. About 1 in 8 had a repeat procedure within 4 years.1123218
Side effects
Bleeding, infection, blood clots, and harm to nearby organs, which are not common. Scar tissue forms inside the belly in most people, and it can make organs stick together. It often causes no symptoms, but it can cause pain or block the bowel. Up to 1 in 5 people have a hernia at the scar after open surgery.12333435
Hysterectomy

Surgery that removes the whole uterus.13

How invasive is it?
Through the vagina, small cuts, or a larger cut in the belly. Recovery takes about 2 to 6 weeks.13
How well does it work?
The only treatment that keeps fibroids from returning. Periods stop.312
Side effects
Bleeding, infection, blood clots, and harm to nearby organs. Menopause begins if the ovaries are also removed. Scar tissue can make organs stick together and, rarely, block the bowel. Up to 1 in 5 people have a hernia at the scar after open surgery.13333435

What are fibroids?

Fibroids are muscle growths in or on the wall of the uterus. They are very common: by age 50, between 2 and 8 in 10 women have them.3

Symptoms can include heavy or long periods, tiredness from blood loss, pelvic pain or pressure, frequent urination, and pain during sex. See a doctor if you have any of these, or if you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for 2 to 3 hours.1336

What should I ask my doctor?
  • Which options could work for me, and which could not?
  • How would each option affect a future pregnancy?
  • How long would I need to recover?
  • Do you offer embolization, or can you refer me to someone who does?
Common questions

Do I need treatment?

Not always. Fibroids that cause no symptoms or only mild ones can simply be watched with exams or an ultrasound. They often shrink after menopause.13

Are fibroids cancer?

No. Rarely, a growth that looks like a fibroid is a hidden cancer. Estimates differ: less than 1 in 1,000, or about 1 in 225 to 1 in 580 people who have fibroid surgery.337

Can I get pregnant after treatment?

It depends on the treatment and your situation. If you hope to get pregnant, ask your doctor which options fit you before you choose. Pregnancy is not possible after a hysterectomy and can be dangerous after endometrial ablation.1230

Do I need a hysterectomy for fibroids?

Not always. Many people never need one. It is the only treatment that keeps fibroids from returning, but medicines, less invasive treatments, and surgery that removes only the fibroids are all options.13
