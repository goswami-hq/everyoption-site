Knee arthritis treatments compared, side by side

GAE vs. knee replacement: strong evidence shows anti-inflammatory pills ease knee pain and most new knees last a long time. Good evidence suggests exercise helps you use your knee better. Early results for genicular (knee) artery embolization (GAE) are encouraging, though doctors are still learning how much of the relief comes from the treatment itself.

How to read this page

This page is for readers who want the studies behind the short guide. Each treatment starts with the conclusion and how sure we are, in the same certainty words the short guide uses. Then come the study sizes, designs, comparison groups, follow-up, harms and how they were counted, who was studied, funding, and what is not known.

Numbers from different studies are not head-to-head comparisons. They come from different people, follow-up times, and outcome measures, so this page never lines them up as if they did.

A number read only in a study's abstract, not its full paper, is marked (abstract only).

Every Option's physician performs knee embolization. It is held to the same standard of evidence as every other option here.

What is knee arthritis?

In arthritis, the cushion between the bones wears away. The bones can rub together. This causes pain, swelling, and stiffness. Arthritis is the most common disease of the knee.1, 2

Common symptoms1

  • Pain when you walk or put weight on the knee
  • Swelling
  • Stiffness after waking, often 30 minutes or less
  • Later, pain at rest or at night
When to see a doctor

See a doctor if your symptoms bother you or get worse. A doctor can check what is causing your symptoms and go over your options.1

Exercise vs. medicines

These are the first steps for most people. No procedure is needed.

Exercise and weight loss

How well it works
Good evidence suggests Better use of the knee; pain 8.7 points better than placebo on a 100-point scale, under the 12-point smallest change that matters
What it involves
An exercise program, often with a physical therapist
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Strengthening and range-of-motion exercise, usually guided by a physical therapist, plus weight loss for people who are overweight. It is the usual first step.1

Bottom line

Good evidence suggests exercise improves knee function. Its effect on pain is small, of uncertain clinical importance on its own, and larger when weight loss is added.3, 4

The studies

  • A Cochrane review found pain 8.7 points better on a 100-point scale than placebo or attention control (low certainty), 13.1 points better than no treatment (low certainty), and 10.4 points better when added to another treatment (moderate certainty), against a 12-point minimal important difference. Function improved with moderate certainty.3
  • In a publicly funded trial of 454 overweight or obese adults aged 55 and over, followed 18 months, diet plus exercise gave 10.6 kg (11.4%) weight loss and less pain (3.6 on a 0 to 20 scale) than exercise alone (4.7) or diet alone (4.8) (abstract only).4

Harms, and how they were counted

Pain can make it hard to keep active; ask for a routine you can keep up.1

Who was studied, and who it is for

A patient source advises reassessing the plan if physical therapy has not helped after several weeks. That is a reason to review the program with a therapist, not to stop being active.1

Pain relievers

How well it works
Strong evidence shows Anti-inflammatory pills ease knee pain; acetaminophen adds very little
What it involves
Pills or skin gels
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Acetaminophen, anti-inflammatory pills (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen and naproxen, and NSAID gels on the skin.1

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows anti-inflammatory pills ease knee pain, and gels help some people with fewer side effects. Acetaminophen adds very little. None changes the joint.1, 5, 6

The studies

  • A network meta-analysis of randomized trials compared NSAIDs and opioids in knee and hip arthritis.5
  • A meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials found acetaminophen's effect on arthritis pain too small to matter for most people.6

Harms, and how they were counted

Do not take more than 4,000 mg of acetaminophen a day. Anti-inflammatory pills raise the risk of heart attack, stroke, and stomach bleeding, so regular use should be discussed with a doctor.1, 7

Other medicines

How well it works
Good evidence suggests Duloxetine eases long-term pain a little; opioids only slightly
What it involves
Daily prescription pills
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Duloxetine, a prescription pill also used for depression and nerve pain; and opioid pills, including tramadol.1

Bottom line

Good evidence suggests duloxetine eases long-term knee pain a little. Opioids ease arthritis pain only slightly.8– to 10

The studies

  • A meta-analysis of randomized trials of duloxetine in osteoarthritis found a small benefit, with more people stopping for side effects than with placebo.8
  • Cochrane reviews of opioids and of tramadol found small benefits; most tramadol trials were industry funded.9, 10

Harms, and how they were counted

Side effects make some people stop duloxetine. For most people with arthritis, opioid harms outweigh the small benefit.5, 8, 9

GAE vs. shots and nerve ablation

These are done without surgery. You keep your own knee. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Knee artery embolization (GAE)

How well it works
Early results are encouraging Pain fell about 37 points on a 100-point scale at 12 months, in studies mostly without a comparison group (abstract only); against a sham, not clearly better when the blinded trials are pooled
What it involves
A thin tube through a tiny skin nick to the knee arteries
Recovery
Most go home the same day
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Genicular artery embolization (GAE) blocks small abnormal vessels that grow into the inflamed lining of the knee (synovium). An interventional radiologist passes a catheter, usually from the groin, under local anesthetic or light sedation, and injects small particles into the genicular arteries feeding those vessels. Most people go home the same day. Less synovial inflammation and fewer nerve endings beside the new vessels is the proposed mechanism, not an established one.17, 30– to 32

Bottom line

Early results are encouraging. Studies without a comparison group consistently report large drops in pain that last at least a year. But in the blinded trials, people who had a sham (fake) procedure also improved substantially, and pooled together GAE was not clearly better than sham. Larger trials are under way.11– to 13

The studies

  • Sham-controlled trials, pooled. Three sham-controlled trials have reported, 138 people in all, measured at different times (1, 4 and 12 months). Pooled, the pain difference was a standardized mean difference of -0.23 (95% CI -0.71 to 0.25): the interval allows a moderate true benefit or none (abstract only).11
  • The first and smallest sham trial. 21 people with mild to moderate arthritis, several centres, single-blind, randomized 2 to 1. At 1 month, pain on a 100 mm scale fell 50.1 mm more with GAE than with sham (95% CI 29.0 to 72.3). Every sham patient then crossed over to GAE, so there is no blinded comparison after 1 month; 5 people were excluded for taking more pain medicine. Its grant list names a device maker (abstract only).14
  • An early-arthritis sham trial. 59 people with early arthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence grade 2), one centre, triple-blind, 12 months. No significant difference on the main or secondary outcomes: knee pain (KOOS) improved 41.3% with GAE and 29.4% with sham. In a subgroup whose arteries were fully embolized (17 people, against 29 controls), 76.5% against 37.9% felt moderately or much better; that subgroup was not randomized, so it is a lead, not a result. Funding is not stated in the record (abstract only).15
  • The largest completed sham trial. 58 people (29 GAE, 29 sham), Kellgren-Lawrence grade 1 to 3, one centre and one operator, double-blind, no dropouts. At 4 months, knee pain (KOOS, 0 to 100) improved 21.4 points with GAE and 18.4 with sham, a difference of 3.0 (95% CI -7.1 to 13.0). Both groups passed the 12-point minimal important difference. 3 of 29 people in the GAE group had no target vessels and were not embolized but were analysed as GAE, as the trial design requires. Grant support includes a device maker.16
  • The same trial at 12 months: a difference of 7.46 points (95% CI -13.63 to 28.56), not significant; synovitis on MRI, assessed to 4 months, did not change. The authors read this as a sustained placebo effect; the upper end of the interval still includes a benefit above the 12-point minimal important difference, and published letters dispute the reading.17
  • A randomized trial without a sham. 50 people (Kellgren-Lawrence grade 2 to 4), GAE plus usual care against usual care alone, randomized but not blinded. Technical success was 82.8% of knees (24 of 29). At 1 year, pain on a visual scale improved 72.7% against 24.2%. A pain flare of about 2 weeks followed in 7 of 28 embolized knees, and one superficial abscess needed treatment. With no sham group, this cannot separate the procedure from placebo (abstract only).18
  • Studies without a comparison group cannot separate the treatment from placebo; read them beside the sham results above. Pooled: 45 studies and 2,205 patients, mostly without a comparison group: pain fell about 37 points on a 100-point scale at 12 months, with certainty rated very low to low (abstract only).12
  • A U.S. single-centre study. 40 people with moderate to severe arthritis at one U.S. centre: 18 of 38 (47.4%) had at least a 50% drop in WOMAC score at 2 years. Of the 25 who responded at 1 year, 18 (72.0%) still responded at 2 years and 7 (28.0%) relapsed. Grant support includes a device maker (abstract only).19
  • A UK single-group study. 46 people treated with permanent beads in the UK: average pain on a 100-point scale fell from 58.6 to 37.7 at 2 years, but only 28 of 46 had 2-year data. The total MRI damage score rose (worse) while synovitis fell. Funded by a device maker.20
  • A Japanese single-group study. 72 people (95 knees) in Japan, Kellgren-Lawrence grade 1 to 3, mostly a temporary embolic agent: cumulative clinical success 86.3% at 6 months and 79.8% at 3 years, with success not defined in the abstract (abstract only).21

Harms, and how they were counted

Temporary skin color change or redness over the knee affects about 1 to 3 out of 10 people (10% to 30% in the society's summary of more than 1,000 reported cases), usually fading within a month. The society's summary puts it at about 0.3% get a small skin ulcer and fewer than 0.1% a focal bone infarct on MRI, generally without symptoms; most reports find no permanent nerve injury, joint infection or bone death.12, 22, 23

In the UCLA cohort treated with 100-micron permanent beads, 7 of 40 had a small skin ulcer that healed on its own, and 2 of 40 had a silent bone infarct (abstract only). A 2026 meta-analysis found focal bone death without symptoms reported only with permanent microspheres, and pooled minor adverse events of 18.3% (abstract only). In the 58-person sham trial there were 23 mild adverse events with GAE and 5 with sham; 11 of 29 had brief skin redness (gone in 1 to 4 weeks), and 2 of 29 had numbness or tingling at the outer ankle, which in one had resolved at 8 months and in the other was still present at 12 months. A German national registry recorded 11 complications in 2,335 procedures (0.5%) at 109 centres; its authors call this a lower limit, because the registry was not built to catch complications (abstract only).11, 16, 17, 24, 25

Embolization studies look for and report skin changes that surgical series rarely list, and knee replacement harms are defined differently, so these rates are not comparable with surgical complication rates.

Who was studied, and who it is for

Trials usually required age over 40, pain above 4 out of 10, and at least 3 months of failed conservative care. They excluded significant artery disease in the legs, pain coming from the spine, and fibromyalgia. In severe (grade 4) arthritis, some studies found lower effectiveness and others equal results.22

The sham trials enrolled mild to moderate knees, where uncontrolled studies look best and placebo responses are large; people having GAE in the U.S. are often older, with more severe arthritis, or cannot have or do not want surgery, so the trials may not represent them. That is an inference: the papers do not report it.15, 16, 22

On who does best, uncontrolled studies conflict. In one 33-person study, 81.8% with mild X-ray changes responded at 6 months against 36.4% with moderate to severe changes (abstract only). Yet the null sham trials were in exactly those milder knees, so the difference may be largely placebo.16, 26

What experts say

The Society of Interventional Radiology says GAE should be offered to people with knee arthritis pain who have failed conservative therapy and are ineligible for, unwilling to undergo, or seeking to delay knee replacement. Most of its authors disclose device-industry consulting; it does not cite the larger null sham trial, and it calls for larger randomized trials.22

What is not known

Whether GAE beats sham in adequately sized trials. Trials under way include sham-controlled trials of 110 people (enrolled), 130 and 40, a three-arm trial of 150 against nerve treatment and sham, trials against a steroid shot, and a 117-person trial against observation.22

Whether it delays knee replacement: no study has measured time to replacement against a randomized control. In U.S. insurance claims, knee replacement within 12 months was 13.1% after GAE and 12.8% in matched people who did not have it, no difference; a 12-month claims comparison cannot show whether surgery is delayed (abstract only).27

Repeat treatment: about 8% had a repeat GAE over up to 2 years in a pooled analysis of 9 studies (270 patients) (abstract only). Which embolic agent is best: The one randomized comparison of embolic agents, 60 people with mild to moderate arthritis followed 12 months, found no difference between a temporary agent and permanent microspheres (pain 3.95 against 3.88 out of 10 at 1 year) (abstract only). Whether it slows arthritis: in one single-group study the MRI damage score worsened.20, 28, 29

Steroid shot

How well it works
Studies disagree Relief for a while, but the relief usually fades by about 3 months
What it involves
A shot during an office visit
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

A corticosteroid injected into the knee during an office visit.1

Bottom line

Studies disagree on how much a steroid shot helps. Relief is real in the short term, but the relief usually fades by about 3 months, repeat shots do not add relief, and physical therapy did better over a year in one trial.1, 33– to 35

The studies

  • A Cochrane review of 27 trials (1,767 people) found a moderate benefit at 1 to 2 weeks (standardized mean difference -0.48) that shrank to -0.22 at 13 weeks and -0.07 at 26 weeks. Most trials were small and of low quality (abstract only).33
  • In a publicly funded trial of 140 people at one centre, shots every 3 months for 2 years thinned cartilage more than saline (-0.21 mm against -0.10 mm) without easing pain more (-1.2 against -1.9). The saline group also improved (abstract only).34
  • In a trial of 156 people, physical therapy beat steroid shots at 1 year (WOMAC difference 18.8, 95% CI 5.0 to 32.6) (abstract only).35

Harms, and how they were counted

Repeat shots may thin the cartilage a little faster (same trial as above). Before a knee replacement: Across 8 retrospective cohorts, a shot within 3 months before knee replacement was linked to more joint infection (risk ratio 1.26, 95% CI 1.18 to 1.35); no link was found beyond 3 months.34, 36

Gel shots (hyaluronic acid)

How well it works
Strong evidence shows Pain about 2 points better than placebo on a 100-point scale, too little to notice (abstract only)
What it involves
Shots during office visits
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

A hyaluronic acid gel injected into the knee, meant to supplement the joint fluid that thins in arthritis.32

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows gel shots ease pain only slightly more than placebo shots, by too little for most people to notice.37

The studies

  • A review of 169 trials (21,163 people) found that in the 24 large trials (8,997 people) pain differed from placebo by about 2 mm on a 100 mm scale (standardized mean difference -0.08), below the minimal important difference. Serious adverse events were more common with gel shots (risk ratio 1.49, 95% CI 1.12 to 1.98) (abstract only).37

Harms, and how they were counted

Brief soreness or swelling of the knee. Serious adverse events were more common than with placebo in the same review.32, 37

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

How well it works
Studies disagree It may ease pain for up to 6 months; no better than saline at 12 months in the largest placebo-controlled trial (abstract only)
What it involves
A blood draw, then shots
Recovery
None
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) is prepared from your own blood and injected into the knee.32, 39

Bottom line

Studies disagree on whether PRP helps. A review of smaller trials suggests it may ease pain for up to 6 months, with low certainty, while the largest placebo-controlled trial found no difference from saline at one year.38, 39

The studies

  • The largest placebo-controlled trial, 288 people aged 50 and over with Kellgren-Lawrence grade 2 to 3 arthritis, found pain changed -2.1 with PRP and -1.8 with saline at 12 months (difference -0.4, 95% CI -0.9 to 0.2), well below the 1.8-point minimal important difference. It tested one leukocyte-poor product (abstract only).39

Harms, and how they were counted

Side effects were mild and short-lived in the largest trial.39

Nerve ablation

How well it works
Studies disagree It may ease pain for about 3 months; no different from a sham at 12 months in one double-blind trial
What it involves
Heat applied to the knee's pain nerves
Recovery
Not well reported
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Radiofrequency heat applied to the genicular nerves that carry pain from the knee. Our sources do not describe the procedure steps or recovery well enough to state them.32

Bottom line

Studies disagree on whether it helps. Pooled trials suggest it may ease pain for about 3 months, but longer relief has not been shown, and the one long sham-controlled trial was negative.40, 41

The studies

  • A review of 25 trials rated the certainty low to very low (abstract only).40
  • A double-blind sham-controlled trial of 64 people found no difference at 12 months.41
  • Against a single steroid shot, not a sham, a trial of 151 people found 74.1% against 16.2% had at least half their pain gone at 6 months (abstract only).42

Harms, and how they were counted

Serious problems seem no more common than with a sham procedure.40

Why the comparison group matters

Knee pain improves a great deal with placebo, and more with procedures than with pills. This is not special to embolization. In placebo-controlled trials, saline did nearly as well as repeated steroid shots over two years and as PRP at one year, gel shots did little better than placebo, and sham surgery matched arthroscopic washout.34, 37, 39, 43

So a large improvement in a study without a comparison group tells you people felt better, not how much of that came from the treatment itself.11

Embolization for other joints

None of these studies had a sham group, and no knee number applies to another joint.44– to 46

Hip. A single-group study followed people for a year, and a non-randomized comparison studied people who could not have surgery.44, 45

Hand and fingers. One study of 9 people with no comparison group (abstract only).46

Shoulder. A health technology assessment found no studies of embolization for shoulder arthritis.47

Frozen shoulder is not arthritis, and it often improves on its own, though this can take up to 3 years. Single-group studies and a meta-analysis report less night pain within weeks; rare serious problems, such as brief loss of vision, have been reported.48– to 50

Knee replacement vs. other surgery

Surgery replaces the worn parts of the knee or changes how weight rests on it.

Knee replacement

How well it works
Strong evidence shows Most new knees last a long time; eased pain more than a non-surgical program at 1 year in the one randomized trial (abstract only)
What it involves
Surgery and a hospital stay
Recovery
Daily activities in 3 to 6 weeks
Keeps your own knee?
No. The worn surfaces are replaced

Surgery replaces the worn cartilage and bone with metal and plastic parts. Surgery takes 1 to 2 hours; most people leave hospital in 1 to 4 days, and most daily activities return in 3 to 6 weeks. Full recovery can take months.56, 59

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows most new knees last a long time: about 8 out of 10 still have their original parts 25 years later (implant survival, not pain relief). For moderate to severe arthritis, one randomized trial found it eases pain more than non-surgical care, but about 1 to 2 out of 10 people are not satisfied (19% of 1,703 knees in an older Ontario study that counted neutral answers; 10% averaged across 21 studies from 2010 to 2022).51– to 54

The studies

  • In the one randomized trial of replacement against a non-surgical program (100 people with moderate to severe arthritis), replacement eased pain more at 1 year, and serious adverse events were more common with surgery (abstract only).54
  • In a parallel trial of people who could have had a replacement, many who did the non-surgical program still had not had surgery 2 years later.55

Harms, and how they were counted

Serious problems such as a joint infection happen in fewer than 2 out of 100 people. Some people have lasting pain or stiffness.56, 57

Who was studied, and who it is for

Timing: for people who have chosen surgery after non-surgical care failed, a 2023 guideline from the American College of Rheumatology and the hip and knee surgeons' society conditionally recommends against delaying surgery to try more non-surgical treatment, on low or very low quality evidence (abstract only).58

Partial knee replacement

How well it works
Results about the same as a full replacement at 5 years, in one randomized trial of 528 people
What it involves
Surgery on one part of the knee
Recovery
Shorter than a full replacement
Keeps your own knee?
Partly

Surgery replaces only the worn compartment of the knee, usually the inner side.1, 60

Bottom line

It is an option when only one part of the knee is worn. In the main trial, results matched a full replacement in the medium term; a partial knee is more likely to need redoing later.51, 60

The studies

  • In a randomized trial of 528 people with arthritis on the inner side of the knee, results at 5 years were about the same with partial and total replacement.60

Harms, and how they were counted

The same kinds of risks as a full replacement: infection, blood clots, and ongoing pain or stiffness. Recovery is shorter: driving after about 3 weeks instead of 6.57, 59

Bone realignment (osteotomy)

How well it works
Early studies suggest Eases symptoms more than non-surgical care, in one trial
What it involves
Surgery to reshape the shin bone
Recovery
Not well reported
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

High tibial osteotomy reshapes the shin bone to shift weight off a worn inner compartment. It is mainly for younger, active people with bow legs.61, 62

Bottom line

Early studies suggest it eases symptoms more than non-surgical care, from one randomized trial at one centre with a parallel preference group.61

The studies

  • Conversion to knee replacement: about 1 out of 10 people have a knee replacement more than 10 years later, in a meta-analysis of follow-up studies.62

Harms, and how they were counted

Our sources do not give its complication rate or recovery time; ask the surgeon.62

Knee scope clean-out

How well it works
Strong evidence shows Does not help knee arthritis; a major guideline advises against it
What it involves
Camera surgery through small cuts
Recovery
Not well reported
Keeps your own knee?
Yes

Arthroscopic washout or trimming (debridement) through small cuts with a camera.1, 43

Bottom line

Strong evidence shows a knee scope clean-out does not help knee arthritis. A major orthopedic guideline advises against it.43, 63, 64

The studies

  • A sham-surgery trial found washout and debridement no better than a sham operation; all groups improved.43
  • A second randomized trial found no added benefit over physical and medical therapy.63

Harms, and how they were counted

It carries the risks of surgery, including blood clots and infection.65

Does a less invasive step affect later surgery?

Knee arthritis is not a condition where waiting threatens life or limb, and guidelines step from exercise and medicines to injections and then surgery. Function can decline while waiting, though.1, 58

After GAE: In 48 knee replacements done a mean of 512 days after GAE, 8.3% had an adverse event and 4.2% a wound-healing problem, all resolved. There was no control group, and the authors are GAE operators. How well people do after the later surgery has not been studied.66

After a steroid shot: see the steroid section; a shot in the three months before surgery was linked to more infection.36
