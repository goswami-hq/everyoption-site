Chronic pelvic pain from veins: every option

Long-term pelvic pain can have many causes. Sometimes it comes from veins in the pelvis. This page explains every option, starting with treatments that need no surgery.

Pelvic vein treatments are done through a tiny skin nick, without surgery. Nothing is cut out of your body.

What is pelvic congestion syndrome?

Pelvic pain that lasts more than 6 months is called chronic pelvic pain. Sometimes it comes from veins in the pelvis that are wide or squeezed. You may hear the name pelvic congestion syndrome. It is most common in women before menopause who have been pregnant.1– to 3

Common symptoms3

  • A dull ache
  • Worse when you stand
  • Worse late in the day
  • Worse after sex
  • Worse before your period
When to see a doctor

See a doctor about pelvic pain that keeps coming back, since it often has more than one cause, such as endometriosis.4, 5

About pelvic vein embolization

A doctor guides a thin tube into the wide pelvic veins. Tiny coils, plugs, or a medicine close those veins.10

How it works

It is done through a tiny skin nick.10

What is embolization?

Embolization means blocking blood flow on purpose, from inside a blood vessel. A doctor guides a thin tube to the small blood vessels that feed the problem area. Then the doctor releases a material that blocks them.6

For pelvic veins, the doctor uses tiny metal coils, small wire-mesh plugs, a medical glue, a medicine that makes the vein scar shut, or other materials. The coils and plugs stay in place for good.7– to 9

How well does it work?

Early results are encouraging. Many women have less pain, and for some the relief lasts several years. Doctors are still learning how much of this relief comes from the treatment itself. More research is under way. If embolization works for you, it may help you avoid surgery to remove your uterus and ovaries, but we don't know that yet.10– to 14

Side effects

Pain in the belly or back right afterward is common. Serious problems are possible, but rare: about 1 out of 100. Most of these are fixed with another procedure. Pain may come back for some women. Some may need a second treatment.11, 15

Results vary from person to person. Talk to a doctor about whether this is right for you.

Find a doctor for pelvic vein treatment

Long-term pelvic pain is common in women. Sometimes it comes from veins in the pelvis that are wide or squeezed. Embolization is one option. You can also consider medicines or surgery. The right choice depends on your symptoms and goals.1, 2, 16

If you have severe pain, heavy bleeding, a fever, a swollen and painful leg, or feel very sick or faint, call 911.

Your other pelvic congestion treatment options

Medicines

These can ease pain. No procedure is needed.

Hormone medicines

These are hormone pills or shots. Your doctor prescribes them.17

How invasive is it?
No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests they ease pain while you take them. However, pain often comes back after you stop.17, 18
Side effects
Weight gain and bloating are common.17
Vein pills (flavonoids)

These are plant-based pills used for vein problems.19

How invasive is it?
No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest they may ease pain.19
Side effects
Side effects are few. Some people get a headache, nausea, or an upset stomach.20
Less invasive treatments

These are done through a tiny skin nick, without surgery. Nothing is cut out of your body. Before you agree to surgery, ask about these.

Pelvic vein embolizationDetails above
Opening a squeezed vein with a stent

If a large vein in the pelvis is squeezed, a doctor can place a stent, a small mesh tube, to hold it open. The stent stays in for good.21

How invasive is it?
It is done through a tiny skin nick. Nothing is cut out of your body.21
How well does it work?
Studies disagree on how often a stent alone eases pelvic pain. Some women also need embolization afterward. Most of what we know about stents is about leg pain and swelling, not pelvic pain. A squeezed vein is common in people with no symptoms, so it is not always the cause.22– to 25
Side effects
Back pain after a stent is common. A clot in the stent or the stent moving is rare, about 1 to 2 out of 100. Doctors may suggest a blood thinner during a later pregnancy.26, 27
Surgery

Surgery closes the wide veins or removes the organs they drain.

Removing the uterus and ovaries

A surgeon removes the uterus and both ovaries.5

How invasive is it?
This is major surgery.
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it can ease pain. However, some women may still have some pain afterward.5
Side effects
You can no longer get pregnant. Removing both ovaries causes menopause, so doctors often give hormone replacement. It has the risks of any major surgery.5
Surgery to close the ovarian vein

A surgeon ties off and cuts the wide ovarian vein.28

How invasive is it?
This is surgery.
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it can ease pain.28
Side effects
It has the risks of any surgery.

How can veins cause pelvic pain?

Veins carry blood back to the heart. In the pelvis, veins near the ovaries and uterus can widen and let blood flow the wrong way. A large vein can also be squeezed by an artery, so blood backs up into the pelvic veins. A vein near the kidney can be squeezed too. Doctors now group these vein problems together.2

How is it found?

Doctors may use an ultrasound, a CT or MRI scan, or a dye test of the veins called a venogram.3, 4, 29

Wide or squeezed veins are common on scans in women who have no pain. So a scan alone does not prove your veins cause your pain. Pelvic pain often has more than one cause, such as endometriosis. Your doctor should check for these too.4, 5, 25, 29, 30

What should I ask my doctor?
  • Could something else, such as endometriosis, be causing my pain?
  • What did my scans show, and do they explain my pain?
  • Which options could work for me, and which could not?
  • Do you offer pelvic vein embolization, or can you refer me to someone who does?
Common questions

Do I need treatment?

Not always. If pain limits your life, talk with your doctor about medicines first. Procedures are an option when medicines do not help.16, 31

Does the pain stop after menopause?

This is often said, but it has not been well studied.32

Can I get pregnant after treatment?

Little is known about pregnancy after embolization. Some women have become pregnant afterward. After a stent, doctors may suggest a blood thinner during pregnancy. Removing the uterus ends the chance of pregnancy.5, 12, 27, 33
