Bleeding hemorrhoids: every option, with and without surgery

Rectal bleeding always needs a doctor's check first, because it is not always hemorrhoids. Sometimes the doctor needs to check your colon.

Hemorrhoids have multiple treatments available. Fiber eases symptoms for many people. Office treatments such as banding also work well. If bleeding keeps coming back, you can look into embolization, which needs no surgery. Surgery is the most lasting fix.

Hemorrhoid embolization treats bleeding hemorrhoids without surgery, through a tiny skin nick in the wrist or groin.

What are hemorrhoids?

Hemorrhoids are swollen veins inside the lower rectum (the last part of the bowel) or under the skin around the anus. Hemorrhoids that are inside the rectum can bleed or bulge out and are usually painless. Outside ones can itch, ache, or form a tender lump.1

Common symptoms1

  • Bright red blood on the toilet paper
  • A lump that bulges out
  • Itching
  • Aching or a tender lump
When to see a doctor

Rectal bleeding needs a check with a doctor first. Bleeding can come from other problems, such as colon cancer or bowel inflammation, so see a doctor about any bleeding. Sudden, severe pain can mean a blood clot in a hemorrhoid or a small tear (fissure), and needs a doctor soon.1, 2

What is hemorrhoid artery embolization (HAE)?

Hemorrhoid artery embolization (HAE) treats bleeding hemorrhoids without surgery. A thin tube goes through a tiny skin nick in the wrist or groin. Tiny coils or beads block the arteries feeding the hemorrhoids, so they bleed less. Most people go home the same day.8– to 10

How it works
What is embolization?

Embolization means blocking blood flow on purpose, from inside a blood vessel. A doctor guides a thin tube to the small blood vessels that feed the problem area. Then the doctor releases a material that blocks them.3

For hemorrhoids, the material is tiny metal coils, tiny plastic beads, or both. Both stay in place for good.4– to 7

If you avoid pork products or have had a reaction to gelatin, tell your doctor before the procedure.7

Recovery

Early studies suggest recovery is far less painful than after surgery in the first days.8

How well does it work?

Early results are encouraging. Most patients bleed less afterwards. About 2 out of 3 people still have little or no bleeding a year later. Some may need a second treatment or surgery. It is not known to fix hemorrhoids that bulge out. It may spare you surgery, but we don't know that yet.10– to 14

Side effects

You may experience increased pressure, an urge to go, or a low fever for a few days. Serious bowel injury is possible, but rare.15– to 19

Results vary from person to person. Talk to a doctor about whether this is right for you.

If bleeding keeps coming back after banding

You have three paths. Banding is a good choice for many people. Embolization needs no surgery, and early studies suggest it hurts much less than surgery in the first days. However, bleeding may come back for some people. Surgery is the most lasting fix, but also the most painful to recover from.2, 8, 12, 20, 21

If your hemorrhoids bulge out and stay bulging out, surgery is usually needed. Embolization is mainly a treatment for bleeding. Studies comparing it with banding are under way. Every Option's physician performs embolization.2, 10, 22– to 24

Hemorrhoids are swollen veins in and around the bottom. They are common. Fiber eases symptoms for many people, and office treatments like banding work well for many. If bleeding keeps coming back after those, embolization is one option that needs no surgery. Surgery is the most lasting fix. The right choice depends on your symptoms and goals.1, 2, 20, 21, 25

If you have heavy bleeding that does not stop, feel faint or dizzy, or have a high fever or severe pain after a procedure, get emergency care or call 911.

Your other hemorrhoid treatment options

Home care

Most people start here. No procedure is needed.

Fiber and home care

This means more fiber from food or a daily fiber supplement and not straining or sitting a long time on the toilet.2

How invasive is it?
No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests fiber eases symptoms and lowers the chance of bleeding. It may not help much with bulging, pain, or itching.25
Side effects
Side effects are mild.25
Creams and warm baths

Creams, wipes, and sitz baths (sitting in warm water) are often used to soothe itching and aching. They treat symptoms, not the hemorrhoid itself.2

How invasive is it?
No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Many people find they ease symptoms for a while, but there is little proof they help in the long run.2
Side effects
Steroid creams should not be used for more than 2 weeks at a time.2
Flavonoid pills

Flavonoids are plant compounds taken as daily pills. Like creams, they ease symptoms rather than remove the hemorrhoid.26, 27

How invasive is it?
No procedure is needed.
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest they ease bleeding and itching. Large reviews disagree on whether they ease pain.26, 27
Side effects
Few safety problems were found in the trials. If you are pregnant, ask your doctor first: a related pill has not been shown safe in pregnancy.27, 28
Less invasive treatments

These need no surgery. Banding is usually tried first, in the office. Embolization is an option when bleeding keeps coming back.

Hemorrhoid artery embolization (HAE)Details above
Banding

A doctor places a tiny rubber band around the base of a hemorrhoid inside the rectum. It cuts off the blood supply, so the hemorrhoid shrinks and falls off.1

How invasive is it?
It is done in the office, with no cut. Many people are back at work the next day.2, 21
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests banding works well for many people. Hemorrhoids come back more often than after surgery. Some people need more than one session.21, 29
Side effects
You may have a dull ache or pressure for a few days and some bleeding.21, 30
Infrared treatment

A small probe uses heat from infrared light to scar the hemorrhoid so it shrinks.1

How invasive is it?
It is done in the office, with no cut.2
How well does it work?
It helps about as much as banding at first. Studies disagree on whether it lasts as long.2, 29, 30
Side effects
It hurts less than banding afterward. Bleeding afterward is about the same.30
Injection (sclerotherapy)

A doctor injects a medicine into the hemorrhoid so it scars and shrinks.31

How invasive is it?
It is done in the office, with no cut.31
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it works about as well as other treatments it was compared with.31
Side effects
Problems afterward are less common than after other treatments.31
Surgery

Surgery ties off, lifts, shrinks, or removes the hemorrhoids. It is the most lasting fix, and recovery hurts more.

Artery tying (ligation)

A surgeon uses an ultrasound probe inside the anus to find the arteries that feed the hemorrhoids, then ties them off with stitches. Nothing is cut out.33

How invasive is it?
This is surgery, with no cut in the skin. Recovery is faster than after standard surgery.32
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it helps, but hemorrhoids come back more often than after standard surgery. It may last longer than banding.32, 33
Side effects
It hurts more than banding in the first week.33
Stapled surgery

A surgeon uses a stapling tool to lift the hemorrhoids back into place.34

How invasive is it?
This is surgery.
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests symptoms, mostly bulging, come back more often than after standard surgery.34
Side effects
Over the long run, pain and bowel control are about the same as after standard surgery.34
Laser surgery

A surgeon shrinks the hemorrhoids with heat from a laser.35

How invasive is it?
This is surgery. People got back to daily life sooner than after standard surgery.35
How well does it work?
Early studies suggest it hurts less than standard surgery. How long it lasts is not yet known.35
Side effects
Bleeding and trouble peeing afterward seem less common than after standard surgery.35
Hemorrhoid removal (hemorrhoidectomy)

A surgeon cuts out the hemorrhoids.20

How invasive is it?
This is surgery. The first week or more is painful, and many people need more than a week off work.20, 21
How well does it work?
Good evidence suggests it is the most lasting fix. Hemorrhoids come back less often than after banding or other procedures.20, 21
Side effects
Pain, bleeding, and trouble peeing afterward are more common than after office treatments. Longer-lasting trouble holding stool is uncommon, about 2 or 3 out of 100.20, 36

Is embolization right for me?

If bleeding keeps coming back after fiber and office treatments and you do not want surgery, it may suit you. Early studies suggest some people also have less aching afterward.8, 11, 22

It is usually not the right choice if your hemorrhoids bulge out and stay out, or if your main problem is sudden, severe pain from a clot or a tear. It has not been studied in pregnancy.2, 23

Serious problems after any hemorrhoid treatment

A serious infection in the pelvis is very rare after any hemorrhoid treatment. Get care right away if you have a fever, severe pain, or feel very sick after a procedure.2

What should I ask my doctor?
  • Have you checked my colon? What is causing the bleeding?
  • Do my hemorrhoids bulge out? Do they go back in?
  • Could banding or another office treatment work for me?
  • If I choose embolization, how often does bleeding come back? What happens if it does?
  • What is the plan if a problem happens after the procedure?
Common questions

Does insurance cover hemorrhoid embolization?

Coverage varies by plan. Some insurers cover it under set conditions, some list it as unproven or not covered, and Medicare decides case by case. Ask your plan before you book. See what each insurer's policy says.37– to 40

Which doctor treats hemorrhoids?

Family doctors, gastroenterologists (digestive specialists), and colorectal surgeons check and treat hemorrhoids, and many do banding in the office. Interventional radiologists perform embolization. Ask your doctor for a referral, or contact one of the doctors listed on our site.

Will hemorrhoids go away on their own?

Sometimes. Some people with an early flare have no more flares for years, but for many the symptoms come back.41

What if I am pregnant?

Hemorrhoids are common in pregnancy. Treatment is usually fiber, treating constipation, and creams. Office treatments can be considered after the birth if symptoms last.2
